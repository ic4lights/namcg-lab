\chapter[Dissipative drag]{Irreversible Entropic Stress and Dissipative Drag}
\label{ch:dissipation}

\paragraph{Learning outcomes.}
After this chapter the reader should be able to:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item distinguish the conservative force from the static
        \(U_{\mathrm{ent}}\) from a proposed, phenomenological
        dissipative component;
  \item state the passivity condition under which a retarded
        low-frequency response gives nonnegative dissipation;
  \item implement and analyze the working law
        \(\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{diss}}=-\eta\mu\mathbf{v}/d^4\)
        as a reduced laboratory model, without treating it as a
        microscopic derivation;
  \item compare that assumed channel with quadrupole
        gravitational-wave losses under stated model conditions;
  \item distinguish the ODE's energy-bookkeeping identity from a
        derivation of mutual-information energy or covariant stress
        conservation.
\end{enumerate}

Chapter~13 used only the static extra density. A time-dependent
configuration can change \(I(A:B)\), but that change alone does not
establish irreversible work or a velocity-odd stress. Neither the
first law nor the static mutual-information potential determines a
retarded response by itself. The drag discussed in this chapter is
a phenomenological component unless it is derived from microscopic
dynamics, as required by Problem~\ref{sec:status-I}.

\section{Reversible versus irreversible}
\label{sec:ch14-split}

On a static spatial slice the mutual-information potential
\eqref{eq:Uent-def} is a function of the instantaneous regions
\(A\) and \(B\). Its metric variation is conservative: the
associated force derives from \(U_{\mathrm{ent}}\) and reverses
under \(\mathbf{v}\to-\mathbf{v}\).

If the regions change in time---because two cores inspiral,
because a star is sheared across a tidal radius, because a
condensate defect is stretched---then in general
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:I-dot}
  \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}I(A:B)
  \neq 0.
\end{equation}
A changing \(I(A:B)\) records a change in the correlations of the
state. It does not, by itself, establish irreversible work or a
force odd under time reversal.

\begin{definition}[Phenomenological entropic-stress split]
  \label{def:Tirrev}
  For bookkeeping, write
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:split-rev-irrev}
    T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{ent}}
    =T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{ent,rev}}
     +T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{ent,irrev}}.
  \end{equation}
  The first term denotes the contribution assigned to the static
  potential \(U_{\mathrm{ent}}\); the second is a proposed
  remainder in the effective description. This split alone does
  not establish that the remainder is dissipative or odd under
  time reversal. Those properties require a dynamical response
  calculation and an appropriate passivity condition.
\end{definition}

Until that derivation is supplied, the split is phenomenological.
Change or production of mutual information along a trajectory is
not, on its own, evidence of irreversibility.

\section{Worldline reduction}
\label{sec:ch14-worldline}

Consider two compact condensed cores with worldlines
\(\gamma_A,\gamma_B\), rest masses \(m_A,m_B\), reduced mass
\(\mu=m_A m_B/(m_A+m_B)\), and separation four-vector
\(r^\mu=x_A^\mu-x_B^\mu\), \(d=\sqrt{r^\mu r_\mu}\) in a local
inertial frame. The entropic action along the worldlines is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:Sent-wl}
  S_{\mathrm{ent}}
  =-\int\mathrm{d}\tau\,U_{\mathrm{ent}}(d,\ell_A,\ell_B,\dot d),
\end{equation}
where \(\ell_A,\ell_B\) are proper core radii and dots are proper
time of the reduced motion. Varying with respect to \(x_A^\mu\)
produces the four-force~\eqref{eq:four-force}.

At leading post-Newtonian order, with \(\ell_A,\ell_B\) frozen,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:force-PN}
  \mathbf{F}_A
  =-\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_A}U_{\mathrm{ent}}(d)
   -\eta^{\mu}(d,\dot d)\,\mathbf{v}
   +O(c^{-2}).
\end{equation}
The first term is Chapter~13. The second term is a phenomenological
drag; it is not obtained by varying the static \(U_{\mathrm{ent}}\),
and the coefficient \(\eta^{\mu}\) is not derived here.
A covariant reduction of such a term would have to satisfy
Proposition~\ref{prop:allowed-drag}, that is, there would have to be
a microscopic entanglement energy \(E_{\mathrm{ent}}\) such that
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:balance-again}
  \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}
  \bigl(E_{\mathrm{mech}}+E_{\mathrm{ent}}\bigr)
  =O(c^{-2}),
\end{equation}
and the force on \(B\) would have to be equal and opposite up to
radiation reaction of the tensor block.

The dissipative response is defined by a conditional Kubo
prescription. At fixed separation \(\mathbf r\), choose a stationary
reference state of the microscopic Hamiltonian \(H(\mathbf r)\) and
define the connected force operator
\(\delta\hat F_i=-\partial H/\partial r_i
-\langle-\partial H/\partial r_i\rangle_{\mathbf r}\). For a
perturbation \(\delta H(t)=-\delta r_j(t)\,\delta\hat F_j\), the
retarded response kernel is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:linear-response}
  \chi^R_{ij}(t)
  =\frac{i}{\hbar}\,\theta(t)\,
   \bigl\langle[\delta\hat F_i(t),\delta\hat F_j(0)]\bigr\rangle_{\mathbf r},
  \qquad
  \chi^R_{ij}(\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i\omega t}
  \chi^R_{ij}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.
\end{equation}
If the reference state is reciprocal and passive and the limit
exists, the low-frequency dissipative tensor is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:Gamma-kubo}
  \Gamma_{ij}
  =\lim_{\omega\downarrow0}
   \frac{\operatorname{Im}\chi^R_{(ij)}(\omega)}{\omega},
  \qquad
  \mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{diss},i}=-\Gamma_{ij}v_j,
\end{equation}
where \(\chi^R_{(ij)}\) is the symmetric part and \(\Gamma_{ij}\) is
positive semidefinite by passivity. This is a conditional
prescription: the NAMCG force correlator, its stationary reference
state, and its passivity have not been calculated.
The causal family \(\chi^R(\omega)=\chi_0/(1-i\omega\tau)\),
\(\tau>0\), has the same static value \(\chi_0\) for every
\(\tau\) but low-frequency slope \(\tau\chi_0\), so a static
susceptibility does not determine the dissipative coefficient.
Positivity of \(I(A:B)\) for each state likewise establishes neither
an ordering between adiabatic and non-adiabatic mutual informations
nor the sign of \(\Gamma\); Problem~\ref{sec:status-I} states the
calculation required.

\section{The working exponent}
\label{sec:ch14-exponent}

Section~\ref{sec:ch6-MI} gave the conformal large-separation tail
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:I-tail}
  I(A:B)
  \;\sim\;
  c_{p_*}
  \left(\frac{\ell_A\ell_B}{d^2}\right)^{p_*},
  \qquad d\gg\ell_A,\ell_B,
\end{equation}
with \(p_*\) and \(c_{p_*}\) and the associated assumptions as in
\eqref{eq:I-falloff}; for a free massless scalar in \(3+1\)
dimensions \(p_*=2\) and the static tail falls as \(d^{-4}\). This
static scaling concerns mutual information only and does not
determine the low-frequency dissipative response. The scalar relation
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:Gamma}
  \Gamma(d)
  =\eta\,\frac{\mu}{d^{4}}
\end{equation}
is therefore a working isotropic laboratory choice, not a
deduction from the static tail or a calculation of the microscopic
response tensor. The coefficient \(\eta\) has not been derived
from a microscopic force-response function. The isotropic scalar
form also requires the longitudinal and transverse components of
\(\Gamma_{ij}\) to agree, or an explicit restriction to radial
motion.

\begin{assumption}[Working dissipative law]
  \label{ass:drag}
  In the reduced two-body laboratory of this book,
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:Fdiss}
    \mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{diss}}
    =-\eta\,\frac{\mu}{d^{4}}\,\mathbf{v},
  \end{equation}
  with \(\eta\ge 0\) constant on a given inspiral and with
  \(E_{\mathrm{ent}}\) defined so that~\eqref{eq:balance-again}
  holds identically.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{ass:drag} is not a multipole theorem. A different
static tail of \(I(A:B)\) does not, by itself, imply a different
drag exponent. The response exponent remains undetermined until the
dynamical calculation in Problem~\ref{sec:status-I} is supplied.
Chapter~16 treats the working exponent as falsifiable;
Chapter~15 integrates the specified laboratory law, not a derived
microscopic response, and no other drag.

For the laboratory bookkeeping, define the accumulated work
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:Wdiss}
  W_{\mathrm{diss}}(t)
  =\int_0^t\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{diss}}\cdot\mathbf{v}\,\mathrm{d}t'.
\end{equation}
Then set
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:Eent-lab}
  E_{\mathrm{ent}}(t)=-W_{\mathrm{diss}}(t)+E_{\mathrm{ent}}(0).
\end{equation}
This definition makes \(E_{\mathrm{mech}}+E_{\mathrm{ent}}\)
constant at the retained order when
\(\dot E_{\mathrm{mech}}=\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{diss}}\cdot\mathbf v\).
It is bookkeeping for the reduced laboratory, not a derivation of
an entanglement energy from \(I(A:B)\). The integrator in
Chapter~15 displays \(E_{\mathrm{mech}}(t)-W_{\mathrm{diss}}(t)\)
as this conserved combination.

\section{Comparison with quadrupole losses}
\label{sec:ch14-quad}

The Isaacson flux of the tensor block for a weak, slow binary is
the Peters--Mathews power
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:Peters}
  P_{\mathrm{GW}}
  =\frac{32}{5}\Geff\mu^2 d^4\Omega^6
   \langle\text{inclination factors}\rangle
\end{equation}
in the usual quadrupole units, up to the precise harmonic
decomposition. The entropic power is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:Pent}
  P_{\mathrm{ent}}
  =-\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{diss}}\cdot\mathbf{v}
  =\eta\mu\frac{v^2}{d^4}.
\end{equation}

For a circular Newtonian orbit, \(v^2=\Geff M/d\) and
\(\Omega^2=\Geff M/d^3\), so
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ratio}
  \frac{P_{\mathrm{ent}}}{P_{\mathrm{GW}}}
  \;\sim\;
  \frac{\eta}{\Geff^3 M^2\mu}\,
  \Bigl(\frac{d}{\Geff M}\Bigr)^{k}
\end{equation}
with a positive power of \(d\) that makes the ratio \emph{grow}
toward wide separations if \(\eta\) is held fixed. That conclusion
is an artefact of treating \(\eta\) as constant down to laboratory
distances. If \(\eta\) is instead suppressed at wide separations, the
growth need not occur; this book does not calculate that dependence,
and a sufficiently small \(\eta\) is required for consistency with
Solar-System tests.
The intended domain of~\eqref{eq:Fdiss} is:

\begin{itemize}
  \item late inspirals of two compact condensed cores, where
        \(d\) is not astronomically large compared with
        \(\ell_A+\ell_B\);
  \item tidal disruption events, where a stellar core is sheared
        at \(d\sim r_{\mathrm{t}}\) and \(\dot d\) is not small;
  \item numerical laboratories that isolate the extra channel.
\end{itemize}

In LIGO-band vacuum inspirals of two objects whose condensate
cores are hidden behind horizons, quadrupole radiation remains
the default dissipative channel. NAMCG does not replace the
Peters formula by~\eqref{eq:Fdiss} in that regime unless
\(\eta\) is independently constrained to be large there. It is
not.

\section{Tidal disruption}
\label{sec:ch14-tde}

A star of radius \(R_*\) and mass \(m_*\) approaching a compact
object of mass \(M\) is disrupted at
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:tidal-radius}
  r_{\mathrm{t}}
  \sim R_*\Bigl(\frac{M}{m_*}\Bigr)^{1/3}.
\end{equation}
Across that radius the baryonic body ceases to be a single region
\(A\) and becomes a collection of debris parcels. The mutual
information between the compact core and the star changes over a
dynamical time
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:tdyn}
  t_{\mathrm{dyn}}
  \sim\sqrt{r_{\mathrm{t}}^3/\Geff M}.
\end{equation}
The non-adiabatic character of this process may motivate testing
the phenomenological drag model near \(r_{\mathrm t}\), but it
does not establish a dissipative response or its magnitude. The
existence and size of any additional drag require the microscopic
dynamics, reference state, and response calculation specified in
Problem~\ref{sec:status-I}. Observable consequences such as
changes to fallback rates or extra heating are therefore
conditional phenomenological possibilities, not predictions
derived in this chapter. They belong to Chapter~16
and require hydrodynamics that this text does not contain.

The book does not simulate debris streams.

\section{Illegal and legal integrators}
\label{sec:ch14-legal}

An integrator that evolves
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:illegal}
  \mu\ddot{\mathbf{r}}
  =-\frac{\Geff M\mu}{d^2}\hat{\mathbf{r}}
   -\gamma\mathbf{v}
\end{equation}
with constant \(\gamma\) and no \(E_{\mathrm{ent}}\) is not a
NAMCG calculation, even if its trajectories look like inspirals.
It dissipates energy into nowhere.

An integrator that evolves
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:legal}
  \mu\ddot{\mathbf{r}}
  =-\nabla U_{\mathrm{N}}(d)
   -\nabla U_{\mathrm{ent}}^{\mathrm{rev}}(d)
   -\eta\frac{\mu}{d^4}\mathbf{v}
\end{equation}
and accumulates~\eqref{eq:Wdiss} is the reduced laboratory
implementation of Assumption~\ref{ass:drag}. Accumulating
\(W_{\mathrm{diss}}\) makes
\(E_{\mathrm{mech}}-W_{\mathrm{diss}}\) constant for the modeled
ODE, up to numerical error. This bookkeeping identity does not
establish a microscopic mutual-information energy or prove that
the ODE is the reduction of a covariantly conserved NAMCG stress.
Chapter~15 implements~\eqref{eq:legal}
with a symplectic Verlet step on the conservative forces and an
explicit treatment of the drag. The gravitational constant used
there is a toy value, not a physical \(G_{\mathrm{eff}}\); that
matching remains open in Problem~F.

\section{Exercises}
\label{sec:ch14-exercises}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Passivity and the sign of drag.}
    For the reciprocal low-frequency response tensor
    \(\Gamma_{ij}(\mathbf r;\ell_A,\ell_B)\) defined in
    Problem~\ref{sec:status-I}, state the passivity condition.
    Show that positive semidefiniteness implies
    \(F_i^{\mathrm{diss}}v_i\leq0\). Explain why
    \(I(A:B)\geq0\) alone implies neither an ordering between
    adiabatic and non-adiabatic states nor a positive drag
    coefficient. Which of the assumptions needed for this Kubo
    conclusion have actually been established for NAMCG?
  \item \textbf{Static versus dynamical scaling.}
    Explain why a power law for static \(I(A:B)\) does not determine
    the separation dependence of the low-frequency drag tensor.
    Identify the dynamical response data needed to calculate that
    dependence.
  \item \textbf{Energy identity.}
    For~\eqref{eq:Fdiss} with circular motion at instantaneous
    radius \(d(t)\), compute \(P_{\mathrm{ent}}\). Compare with
    \(\mathrm{d}U_{\mathrm{N}}/\mathrm{d}t\) along a Peters
    inspiral and estimate a separation where the two powers
    could be comparable for a chosen \(\eta\).
  \item \textbf{Equal and opposite.}
    Show that~\eqref{eq:Fdiss}, written in the reduced-mass
    frame, corresponds to equal-and-opposite three-forces on
    \(A\) and \(B\). What radiation-reaction term would have
    to be added to break that balance, and which block of
    \(T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{total}}\) supplies it?
  \item \textbf{Constant-\(\gamma\) counterexample.}
    Take~\eqref{eq:illegal} and send \(d\to\infty\). What
    happens to the dissipated energy per unit time, and why
    is that incompatible with a mutual-information tail that
    vanishes at infinity?
  \item \textbf{Tidal time.}
    Evaluate~\eqref{eq:tdyn} for a solar-type star at the tidal
    radius of a \(10^6 M_\odot\) compact object. Why is the
    adiabatic approximation of Chapter~13 unreliable there?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Bridge to Chapter~15}
\label{sec:ch14-bridge}

The ordinary-differential laboratory is now specified:
Newtonian gravity, optional reversible extra potential, drag
\eqref{eq:Fdiss}, and an accumulated work~\eqref{eq:Wdiss}.
Chapter~15 integrates that system with a symplectic Verlet step,
plots the trajectory and the energy balance, and states, in the
first paragraph, that the figure is a reduced model. The Python
code that produces the figure lives in \texttt{code/} and is not
a derivation of Einstein gravity. After the figure, Chapter~16
collects every named assumption in the book and lists the
observations that would kill the framework.
