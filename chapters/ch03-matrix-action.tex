\chapter[The microscopic action]{Matrix Coordinates and the Microscopic Action}
\label{ch:matrix-action}

\paragraph{Learning outcomes.}
After this chapter the reader should be able to:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item write the non-commutative coordinate algebra
        \([\hat X^\mu,\hat X^\nu]=i\Theta^{\mu\nu}\) and state how
        \(\Theta^{\mu\nu}\) supplies an ultraviolet cutoff;
  \item write both microscopic actions used in the NAMCG notes---the
        pregeometric matrix action and the covariant condensate-field
        action---and say what each is for;
  \item explain why the order parameter must carry an internal index
        \(I\) and a tangent index \(A\);
  \item expand the Mexican-hat matrix potential about its vacuum and
        identify the radial and Goldstone directions;
  \item enter Chapter~4 knowing which object will condense into a
        vierbein.
\end{enumerate}

Chapter~2 supplied geometry as language. This chapter supplies the
field. After this point the metric is not an independent ingredient.
It is something the field must produce.

\section{The scalar failure, restated as a field-content problem}
\label{sec:ch3-scalar-failure}

A single complex scalar \(\phi\) transforming as a singlet under every
tangent-space representation can condense. It can eat one Goldstone
phase into a gauge field. It cannot furnish a local Lorentz frame.
The infrared excitations of such a condensate are, at best,
\begin{itemize}
  \item a radial Higgs scalar,
  \item a massive or massless vector, if a gauge field is present,
  \item phonons that admit an acoustic metric rewrite.
\end{itemize}
There is no symmetric trace-free tensor \(T_{\mu\nu}\) hiding in
\(\phi\). Seeking that tensor in a more elaborate potential for the
same \(\phi\) is a representation-theoretic error, not a calculational
one.

NAMCG therefore promotes the order parameter to a rectangular (or
square, after a choice of basis) matrix field
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:Phi-field}
  \Phi^I{}_A(x),
\end{equation}
where \(I=1,\ldots,N\) labels an internal gauge representation and
\(A=0,1,2,3\) labels an emergent tangent space. The field is a
linear map from the tangent space of an auxiliary frame into the
internal space, or the reverse, according to index placement. The
placement used in this book is: \(I\) up, \(A\) down on \(\Phi\),
unless a formula from the source notes is being quoted verbatim.

The rest of the chapter writes an action for \(\Phi\) and for a
closely related pregeometric object, the matrix coordinates
\(\hat X^\mu\).

\section{Two presentations, and why both appear}
\label{sec:ch3-two-presentations}

The original notes use two microscopic languages. They are not
identical, and treating them as interchangeable by slogan would
produce a confused book.

\paragraph{Presentation A: pregeometric matrix coordinates.}
There is, at the outset, no manifold. The dynamical objects are
Hermitian operators \(\hat X^\mu\) acting on a Hilbert space
\(\mathcal{H}\). Spacetime points, if they appear at all, appear as
eigenvalue data of a complete commuting subset of these operators, or
as a derived continuum after condensation.

\paragraph{Presentation B: condensate field on an auxiliary background.}
There is an auxiliary manifold with a non-dynamical metric
\(g^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}\). The dynamical object is the field
\(\Phi^I{}_A(x)\). The physical metric \(g_{\mu\nu}\) is not
\(g^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}\). The auxiliary metric is a scaffold for writing
derivatives and heat kernels. Chapter~8 uses this scaffold.

This book treats Presentation~A as the ontological starting point and
Presentation~B as the background-field language appropriate after a
condensate has selected a notion of local coordinates. Chapter~4 is
the bridge. Until that bridge is built, the two actions below are
written separately and not added together.

\begin{assumption}[Auxiliary background is non-dynamical]
  \label{ass:aux-metric}
  Whenever Presentation~B is in use, \(g^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}\) is a fixed
  scaffold, usually taken flat,
  \(g^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}\). It is not varied in the
  Einstein--Hilbert sense. Varying the \emph{emergent} metric
  \(g_{\mu\nu}\) defined in Chapter~4 is a different operation.
\end{assumption}

\section{Presentation A: matrix coordinates and ultraviolet cutoff}
\label{sec:ch3-matrices}

\begin{definition}[Matrix coordinates]
  \label{def:matrix-coords}
  Let \(\mathcal{H}\) be a separable Hilbert space and let
  \(\hat X^\mu\), \(\mu=0,1,2,3\), be Hermitian operators on
  \(\mathcal{H}\). The fundamental kinematic relation of Presentation~A
  is
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:NC}
    [\hat X^\mu,\hat X^\nu]=i\Theta^{\mu\nu},
  \end{equation}
  where \(\Theta^{\mu\nu}=-\Theta^{\nu\mu}\) is an antisymmetric
  tensor with dimensions of length squared. If \(\Theta^{\mu\nu}\) is
  a multiple of the identity on \(\mathcal{H}\), it is a
  \(c\)-number background. In the fully dynamical theory it may
  itself be an operator; the notes treat the leading infrared
  analysis with a \(c\)-number \(\Theta^{\mu\nu}\).
\end{definition}

The scale
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:LUV-def}
  \LUV\sim\Theta^{-1/2},
  \qquad
  \Theta
  \equiv
  \sqrt{\tfrac12\Theta_{\mu\nu}\Theta^{\mu\nu}},
\end{equation}
is the ultraviolet cutoff of the theory. Two operators that fail to
commute cannot be simultaneously diagonalized, so the notion of a
sharply localized event \((x^0,x^1,x^2,x^3)\) is meaningless below
\(\LUV^{-1}\). This is the NAMCG replacement for a hard lattice.

\begin{remark}[What \(\Theta^{\mu\nu}\) is not]
  Equation~\eqref{eq:NC} is not yet a solution of the cosmological-
  constant problem, not yet a black-hole core, and not yet a graviton.
  It is a kinematic regulator. Claims that begin ``because spacetime
  is non-commutative\ldots'' and end at galactic rotation curves are
  skipping the work of Chapters~4--14.
\end{remark}

A Yang--Mills-type matrix action for these coordinates, including a
cubic structure-constant coupling to a background condensate tensor
\(C_{\mu\nu\lambda}\), is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:Smatrix}
  \begin{aligned}
    S_{\mathrm{matrix}}
    &=-\frac{1}{4g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2}
      \mathrm{Tr}\bigl(
        [\hat X^\mu,\hat X^\nu][\hat X_\mu,\hat X_\nu]
      \bigr)
      +\frac{1}{3!}
      \mathrm{Tr}\bigl(
        C_{\mu\nu\lambda}[\hat X^\mu,\hat X^\nu]\hat X^\lambda
      \bigr)\\
    &\qquad
      +S_{\mathrm{matter}}(\Psi,\hat X^\mu).
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The first term is the IKKT / reduced-Yang--Mills kinetic term. On a
background satisfying~\eqref{eq:NC} it is proportional to
\(\mathrm{Tr}(\Theta_{\mu\nu}\Theta^{\mu\nu})\) plus fluctuation
pieces. The cubic term selects a preferred vacuum condensation
structure; its detailed index symmetries are constrained by the
requirement that the vacuum preserve a Lorentzian interpretation
after Chapter~4.

Matter fields \(\Psi\) in \(S_{\mathrm{matter}}\) are functions of the
matrices \(\hat X^\mu\) in the sense of the Weyl map associated with
\(\Theta^{\mu\nu}\). This book does not need the full star-product
calculus until phenomenology with explicit localized sources appears
in Chapter~14. The existence of such a map is enough for now.

\section{Presentation B: the covariant condensate action}
\label{sec:ch3-Phi-action}

Once local coordinates \(x^\mu\) have been introduced as labels on an
auxiliary manifold, the dynamical variable is the matrix field
\(\Phi^I{}_A(x)\). Let \(A_\mu\) be a connection on the internal
bundle, with covariant derivative
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:Dmu-Phi}
  D_\mu\Phi=\partial_\mu\Phi-iA_\mu\Phi
\end{equation}
acting on the \(I\) index. The microscopic action used for the
heat-kernel analysis is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:Smicro}
  S_{\mathrm{micro}}[\Phi,A]
  =\int\mathrm{d}^4x\,\sqrt{-g^{(0)}}\,
   \mathrm{Tr}\Bigl(
    -\,g^{(0)\mu\nu}(D_\mu\Phi)^\dagger D_\nu\Phi
     -V(\Phi^\dagger\Phi)
   \Bigr).
\end{equation}
The trace is over internal indices. Tangent indices on \(\Phi\) are
contracted through the potential and through the identification of
\(A\) with a frame index after condensation; they are not traced
in the same sum.
In the signature \((-,+,+,+)\) of Chapter~2 the kinetic term is written with
the minus sign so that time derivatives have positive energy. This is a
correction to the Presentation-B action as stated; that the matrix action
of Presentation~A reproduces this sign is part of the unproved dictionary
(Assumption~\ref{ass:dictionary}) and remains to be derived.

The potential that triggers condensation is the matrix Mexican hat
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:V}
  V(\Phi^\dagger\Phi)
  =\frac{\lambda}{4}
   \mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\Phi^\dagger\Phi-v^2\mathbb{I}\bigr)^2.
\end{equation}
Its minima lie on the manifold
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:vacuum-manifold}
  \Phi^\dagger\Phi=v^2\mathbb{I}
\end{equation}
in the internal space, up to unitary rotations. A representative
vacuum used throughout the book is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:VEV}
  \langle\Phi^I{}_A\rangle=v\,\delta^I{}_A,
\end{equation}
which is available when the internal dimension is at least four and a
basis has been chosen that identifies four internal directions with
the tangent directions. If \(N>4\), the remaining internal directions
must either decouple or be locked by additional Yukawa-like couplings.
The source notes assume that a vacuum of the form~\eqref{eq:VEV}
exists. This book records that as an assumption.

\begin{assumption}[Existence of a frame-locking vacuum]
  \label{ass:frame-lock}
  There exists a stable vacuum~\eqref{eq:VEV} (or a locally equivalent
  vacuum) in which four internal directions are identified with the
  four tangent directions. Goldstone modes associated with the broken
  generators are available to be eaten by a connection, as in
  Chapter~10.
\end{assumption}

\section{Index structure and the intended infrared spectrum}
\label{sec:ch3-indices}

Under an internal gauge transformation \(\Omega(x)\) and a local
Lorentz transformation \(\Lambda(x)\), the field is required to
transform as
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:Phi-transform}
  \Phi^I{}_A(x)
  \;\longmapsto\;
  \Omega^I{}_J(x)\,\Phi^J{}_B(x)\,\Lambda^B{}_A(x).
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:Phi-transform} is the transformation law that
makes \(\Phi\) capable of locking the two groups to a diagonal
subgroup. It is a choice of field content. It is not derived from
Presentation~A alone; it is the additional structure that Presentation~B
puts in by hand so that a vierbein can exist.

Fluctuations about~\eqref{eq:VEV} will be decomposed in Chapter~5
into
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:split-preview}
  \delta\Phi^I{}_A
  =\frac14\eta_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}\,I
   +A_\mu\gamma^\mu
   +T_{\mu\nu}.
\end{equation}
The only fact needed here is that the last piece exists because
\(\Phi\) has two indices that can be symmetrized and made traceless
after the vacuum has identified \(I\) with \(A\). A scalar has no
such piece.

\section{Equations of motion from Presentation B}
\label{sec:ch3-eom}

Varying~\eqref{eq:Smicro} with respect to \(\Phi^\dagger\) gives
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:Phi-eom}
  D_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g^{(0)}}\,g^{(0)\mu\nu}D_\nu\Phi\bigr)
  -\sqrt{-g^{(0)}}\,
   \frac{\lambda}{2}\Phi
   \bigl(\Phi^\dagger\Phi-v^2\mathbb{I}\bigr)
  =0.
\end{equation}
Varying with respect to \(A_\mu\) produces a matrix current
conservation law
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:gauge-eom}
  D_\mu
  \bigl(
    \Phi(D^\mu\Phi)^\dagger-(D^\mu\Phi)\Phi^\dagger
  \bigr)
  =0,
\end{equation}
up to the conventional factor of \(i\) in the current definition.
These equations determine the condensate profile on the auxiliary
background. They do not yet determine the Einstein tensor of the
emergent metric. That requires integrating out fluctuations, which is
Chapter~8, after the emergent metric has been defined in Chapter~4
and the tensor mode identified in Chapter~5.

\section{Relating the two presentations}
\label{sec:ch3-relate}

A complete derivation from~\eqref{eq:Smatrix} to~\eqref{eq:Smicro}
would construct, from the spectra of \(\hat X^\mu\), a commutative
manifold, a Weyl map, and a slowly varying matrix-valued field on that
manifold whose covariant derivative reproduces the commutator
\([\hat X^\mu,\hat\Phi]\). That derivation is not supplied in full in
the source notes. The working relation used in this book is the
following.

\begin{assumption}[Infrared dictionary]
  \label{ass:dictionary}
  In a condensate phase in which the matrices \(\hat X^\mu\) are,
  to leading order, commutative coordinates \(x^\mu\mathbb{I}\) plus
  fluctuations, there exists a slowly varying field
  \(\Phi^I{}_A(x)\) such that
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:dictionary}
    [\hat X_\mu,\Phi]
    \;\longleftrightarrow\;
    i\Theta_{\mu\nu}D^\nu\Phi
    \qquad\text{plus higher-derivative corrections in \(\Theta\).}
  \end{equation}
  Under this dictionary the quadratic commutator term
  in~\eqref{eq:Smatrix} is required to generate the kinetic term
  in~\eqref{eq:Smicro}, including its sign in the signature
  \((-,+,+,+)\); this is a matching condition, not an established result.
  \(\LUV\) remains the cutoff of the derivative expansion.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{ass:dictionary} is the most important unproved bridge
in the microscopic setup. Chapter~17 lists a sharper derivation of
this dictionary as an open problem. Every later heat-kernel formula
is a calculation \emph{within} Presentation~B, contingent on the
dictionary.

\begin{remark}[Constant-\(\Theta\) Moyal check (conditional; not closure)]
  \label{rem:ch3-moyal-check}
Assume a Moyal realization with constant, real, antisymmetric
\(\Theta^{\mu\nu}\), and define
\[
  (f\star g)(x)
  =f(x)\exp\!\left(
    \frac{i}{2}\Theta^{\mu\nu}
    \overleftarrow{\partial_\mu}\overrightarrow{\partial_\nu}
  \right)g(x).
\]
For general symbols,
\[
  [f,g]_\star
  =i\Theta^{\mu\nu}(\partial_\mu f)(\partial_\nu g)
   -\frac{i}{24}\Theta^{\mu\nu}\Theta^{\rho\sigma}
      \Theta^{\alpha\beta}
      (\partial_\mu\partial_\rho\partial_\alpha f)
      (\partial_\nu\partial_\sigma\partial_\beta g)
   +O(\Theta^5).
\]
For the coordinate symbol \(x_\mu\), derivatives of order greater
than one vanish, so
\[
  [x_\mu,\phi]_\star
  =i\Theta_{\mu\nu}\partial^\nu\phi
\]
exactly in this realization. This verifies the coordinate-background
kinematic identity, but not the full dictionary
\eqref{eq:dictionary} for fluctuating matrix coordinates, a gauge
connection, or nonconstant \(\Theta\).

There is a further restriction if the two-commutator contraction is
to reproduce a Lorentzian kinetic metric. With signature
\((-,+,+,+)\), define
\[
  K_\Theta^{\nu\rho}
  :=\eta_{\alpha\beta}\Theta^{\alpha\nu}\Theta^{\beta\rho},
  \qquad
  N^\mu{}_\nu:=\Theta^{\mu\alpha}\eta_{\alpha\nu}.
\]
Then \(N\) is a real Lorentz generator and
\(K_\Theta^{\nu\rho}=-(N^2)^\nu{}_\sigma\eta^{\sigma\rho}\).
Thus a nonzero metric-proportional contraction
\(K_\Theta^{\nu\rho}=c\,\eta^{\nu\rho}\), \(c\ne0\), would require
\(N^2=-c\,\mathbb I\). In a Lorentz frame, writing \(N\) in terms of
boost and rotation vectors \(\mathbf E,\mathbf B\), its
characteristic polynomial is
\[
  p(z)=z^4+(|\mathbf B|^2-|\mathbf E|^2)z^2
             -(\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B)^2 .
\]
But \(N^2=-c\,\mathbb I\) would give
\(p(z)=(z^2+c)^2\), whose constant term \(c^2>0\), whereas the
displayed Lorentz-generator polynomial has constant term
\(-(\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B)^2\leq0\). Hence no nonzero \(c\) is
possible. If \(c=0\), \(K_\Theta=0\) says that the image of the
antisymmetric map \(\Theta\) is totally null. In Lorentzian
four-space a totally null subspace has dimension at most one, while
the rank of an antisymmetric matrix is even; therefore
\(\Theta=0\). So no nonzero constant real \(\Theta\) contracted with
\(\eta\) yields a tensor proportional to \(\eta^{\nu\rho}\).
For example, if only \(\Theta^{12}=-\Theta^{21}=\theta\) is nonzero,
then \(K_\Theta^{\nu\rho}=\theta^2
\operatorname{diag}(0,1,1,0)\).

This obstruction concerns only the stated constant-\(\Theta\)
contraction. Matching a Lorentzian kinetic term would need a
different specified mechanism, such as a dynamical or
nonconstant background, a fluctuation-generated metric, or an
additional tangent-index contraction from a derived field structure.
It does not rule out those possibilities or an enlarged matrix
construction, and it does not derive the kinetic term, its sign, a
coframe, or local Lorentz symmetry. The matrix-derived criterion of
Problem~D remains open. Problems~B, C, D, F and~G remain open.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Constant-\(\Theta\) trace and quadratic fluctuations
(conditional; not closure)]
  \label{rem:ch3-trace-quadratic}
Assume the irreducible Weyl representation of the constant,
nondegenerate Heisenberg algebra
\[
  [\bar X^\mu,\bar X^\nu]
  =i\Theta^{\mu\nu}\mathbb{I},
\]
and use the Weyl convention
\[
  \hat f
  :=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\,
       \widetilde f(k)e^{ik_\mu\bar X^\mu}.
\]
For Schwartz symbols, the Weyl trace formula and Moyal trace
identity are
\[
  \operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal H}\hat f
  =\frac{1}{(2\pi)^2|\operatorname{Pf}\Theta|}
       \int\mathrm{d}^4x\,f(x),
  \qquad
  \int\mathrm{d}^4x\, f\star g
  =\int\mathrm{d}^4x\,fg .
\]
Thus the constant-\(\Theta\) trace density in this representation is
\((2\pi)^{-2}|\operatorname{Pf}\Theta|^{-1}\).

For the fluctuation calculation, restrict to the
\(C_{\mu\nu\lambda}=0\) branch of~\eqref{eq:Smatrix} and omit matter
fluctuations. The central background is a classical solution of the
pure quartic matrix equation because its commutators are central.
Write
\[
  \hat X^\mu=\bar X^\mu+\hat A^\mu,\qquad
  D^\mu=[\bar X^\mu,\,\cdot\,],\qquad
  f^{\mu\nu}=D^\mu\hat A^\nu-D^\nu\hat A^\mu .
\]
Assume the fluctuation symbols \(a^\mu(x)\) decay rapidly enough for
the trace formula and integrations by parts to apply, so the linear
term vanishes. The quadratic matrix action is
\[
  S_{\mathrm{YM}}^{(2)}
  =-\frac{1}{4g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2}
       \operatorname{Tr}(f_{\mu\nu}f^{\mu\nu}).
\]
The exact constant-\(\Theta\) coordinate identity gives
\[
  f^{\mu\nu}\longleftrightarrow
       i\mathcal F_\Theta^{\mu\nu},\qquad
  \mathcal F_\Theta^{\mu\nu}
  :=\Theta^{\mu\rho}\partial_\rho a^\nu
    -\Theta^{\nu\rho}\partial_\rho a^\mu .
\]
Consequently,
\[
  \boxed{
  S_{\mathrm{YM}}^{(2)}
  =\frac{1}{4g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2(2\pi)^2
                 |\operatorname{Pf}\Theta|}
       \int\mathrm{d}^4x\,
       \mathcal F_{\Theta,\mu\nu}\mathcal F_\Theta^{\mu\nu}}
\]
with indices contracted using \(\eta_{\mu\nu}\). In particular,
\[
  \mathcal F_{\Theta,\mu\nu}\mathcal F_\Theta^{\mu\nu}
  =2K_\Theta^{\rho\sigma}
       \partial_\rho a_\nu\,\partial_\sigma a^\nu
   -2\Theta^{\mu\rho}\Theta_\nu{}^\sigma
       \partial_\rho a^\nu\,\partial_\sigma a_\mu,
  \qquad
  K_\Theta^{\rho\sigma}
  :=\eta_{\mu\nu}\Theta^{\mu\rho}\Theta^{\nu\sigma}.
\]
The second, mixed term is part of the vector kinetic operator; this
is not the scalar operator of
Remark~\ref{rem:ch3-varying-theta}.

Under the linearized matrix-gauge transformation
\[
  \delta a^\mu=\Theta^{\mu\rho}\partial_\rho\lambda,
\]
the antisymmetry of \(\Theta\) and commutativity of partial
derivatives give \(\delta\mathcal F_\Theta^{\mu\nu}=0\). In Fourier
space, writing \(q^\mu=\Theta^{\mu\rho}k_\rho\), the quadratic
kernel is proportional to
\[
  \mathcal M_{\alpha\beta}(k)
  =q^2\eta_{\alpha\beta}-q_\alpha q_\beta,
  \qquad
  \mathcal M_{\alpha\beta}q^\beta=0,
  \qquad q^2=K_\Theta^{\rho\sigma}k_\rho k_\sigma .
\]
For the usual flat scaffold \(g^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}\),
the constant-\(\Theta\) obstruction of
Remark~\ref{rem:ch3-moyal-check} gives
\(K_\Theta\ne c\,g^{(0)}\) for nonzero real \(\Theta\). Thus this
quadratic action does not have the \(g^{(0)}\)-contracted form used
in Presentation~B. Its sign does not establish a healthy Lorentzian
energy.

For \(g^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}\), let
\[
  P(k):=q^2=K_\Theta^{\rho\sigma}k_\rho k_\sigma .
\]
This is a characteristic-level statement for the displayed
quadratic kernel, not a claim about the full gauge-fixed PDE. When
\(q^2\ne0\), \(\mathcal M=q^2\eta-qq\) has rank three with kernel
spanned by \(q\); its determinant on the quotient is proportional
to \((q^2)^3\). When \(q\ne0\) is null,
\(\mathcal M=-qq\) has rank one, so the rank of \(\mathcal M\) drops
to one and its kernel grows to dimension three.

A degenerate example is the rank-two electric block
\[
  \Theta^{01}=E\ne0,\qquad
  \Theta^{\mu\nu}=0
  \quad\text{for all other independent components}.
\]
It gives
\[
  K_\Theta=\operatorname{diag}(E^2,-E^2,0,0),
  \qquad
  P(k)=E^2(k_0^2-k_1^2).
\]
The characteristic roots in scaffold time \(x^0\) are real,
\(k_0=\pm k_1\), but the polynomial is degenerate: it contains no
\(k_2\) or \(k_3\). When \(k_0=k_1=0\), \(q^\mu=0\) and the entire
quadratic vector kernel vanishes, even for nonzero transverse
momenta. This is a characteristic-level observation, not a claim
of well-posedness for a gauge-fixed initial-value problem.

If \(\Theta\) is nondegenerate, \(K_\Theta\) is a nondegenerate
Lorentzian form by congruence. For any \(\eta\)-timelike covector
\(n_\rho\), antisymmetry gives
\(n_\mu\Theta^{\mu\rho}n_\rho=0\), so
\(q^\mu=\Theta^{\mu\rho}n_\rho\) is orthogonal to \(n\).
Since \(\Theta\) is invertible and \(n\ne0\), \(q\ne0\), and hence
\(P(n)=q^2>0\). Therefore no \(\eta\)-timelike covector is a
hyperbolic direction for this characteristic polynomial. The
polynomial does have hyperbolic directions with \(P(n)<0\), but
they are not \(\eta\)-timelike. Neither the degenerate nor
nondegenerate case establishes a healthy Lorentzian energy or a
well-posed gauge-fixed problem.

This is an action for the matrix-coordinate symbol \(a^\mu\),
not for \(\Phi^I{}_A\). It does not construct \(\Phi^I{}_A\), a
coframe, or a tangent-space connection, and makes no claim for
nonconstant \(\Theta\). Problems~B, C, D, F and~G remain open.
\end{remark}
\begin{remark}[A varying-\(\Theta\) Lorentzian toy geometry
(conditional; not a matrix derivation)]
  \label{rem:ch3-varying-theta}
The exact coordinate-commutator identity in
Remark~\ref{rem:ch3-moyal-check} is for constant \(\Theta\) and is
not asserted for the varying bivector used here; a nonconstant
Poisson bivector requires a corresponding deformation-quantization
product, whose coordinate commutators can include derivatives of
\(\Theta\).

As a geometric example, choose by hand
\[
  \Theta
  =a\,\partial_0\wedge\partial_1
   +b(y)\,\partial_2\wedge\partial_3,\qquad
  y=x^2,\qquad b(y)=b_0e^{\kappa y^2},
\]
with real \(a\ne0\) and \(b_0>0\). This is a Poisson bivector:
the two blocks have disjoint coordinates, and \(b\) depends only on a
coordinate within its own two-dimensional block. Its Pfaffian is
\(ab(y)\ne0\). The algebraic tensor
\[
  K_\Theta^{\nu\rho}
  :=\eta_{\alpha\beta}\Theta^{\alpha\nu}\Theta^{\beta\rho}
  =\operatorname{diag}(a^2,-a^2,b(y)^2,b(y)^2)
\]
is built from this chosen \(\Theta\). If, as an additional
normalization and metric-identification assumption, one sets
\[
  K_\Theta^{\nu\rho}
  =\ell_*^4\,\tilde g^{\nu\rho},
\]
then
\[
  \mathrm{d}\tilde s^2
  =\ell_*^4\left[
    a^{-2}(\mathrm{d}x^0)^2-a^{-2}(\mathrm{d}x^1)^2
    +b(y)^{-2}\bigl((\mathrm{d}y)^2+(\mathrm{d}x^3)^2\bigr)
  \right].
\]
In the signature \((+,-,+,+)\) of \(\tilde g\), \(x^1\) is the
odd-signed coordinate. The two-dimensional conformal factor is
\(e^\sigma=\ell_*^2/b(y)\), so this chosen metric has scalar
curvature
\[
  \tilde R(y)
  =-2e^{-2\sigma}\frac{\mathrm{d}^2\sigma}{\mathrm{d}y^2}
  =\frac{4\kappa b_0^2}{\ell_*^4}e^{2\kappa y^2}.
\]
It is nonflat for \(\kappa\ne0\).

For completeness, one orthonormal coframe for this chosen metric is
obtained by setting
\[
  L=\frac{\ell_*^2}{|a|},\qquad
  f(y)=\frac{\ell_*^2}{b(y)}>0,\qquad
  \eta_{\hat a\hat b}
  =\operatorname{diag}(-,+,+,+),
\]
and choosing
\[
  \tilde e^{\hat 0}=L\,\mathrm{d}x^1,\qquad
  \tilde e^{\hat 1}=L\,\mathrm{d}x^0,\qquad
  \tilde e^{\hat 2}=f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y,\qquad
  \tilde e^{\hat 3}=f(y)\,\mathrm{d}x^3.
\]
The swap of the first two coordinate labels assigns the negative
frame direction to \(x^1\). This coframe obeys
\[
  \tilde g_{\mu\nu}
  =\eta_{\hat a\hat b}\,
    \tilde e^{\hat a}{}_\mu\tilde e^{\hat b}{}_\nu
\]
and reproduces the displayed metric. It is one choice; other
orthonormal coframes are related locally by Lorentz transformations
preserving \(\eta_{\hat a\hat b}\). This freedom follows from the
metric description and is not a gauge symmetry derived from the
matrix action.

Define the torsion-free, metric-compatible spin connection by
\[
  \mathrm{d}\tilde e^{\hat a}
  +\tilde\omega^{\hat a}{}_{\hat b}
       \wedge\tilde e^{\hat b}=0,\qquad
  \tilde\omega_{\hat a\hat b}
  =-\tilde\omega_{\hat b\hat a},
\]
with curvature convention
\[
  \tilde{\mathcal R}^{\hat a}{}_{\hat b}
  =\mathrm{d}\tilde\omega^{\hat a}{}_{\hat b}
   +\tilde\omega^{\hat a}{}_{\hat c}\wedge
    \tilde\omega^{\hat c}{}_{\hat b}.
\]
Writing \(q=f'/f=-2\kappa y\), the only nonzero connection
one-forms are
\[
  \tilde\omega^{\hat 3}{}_{\hat 2}
       =q\,\mathrm{d}x^3,\qquad
  \tilde\omega^{\hat 2}{}_{\hat 3}
       =-q\,\mathrm{d}x^3,
\]
or, with both frame indices raised,
\(\tilde\omega^{\hat 2\hat 3}=2\kappa y\,\mathrm{d}x^3\).
The only independent curvature two-form is
\[
  \tilde{\mathcal R}^{\hat 2}{}_{\hat 3}
  =2\kappa\,\mathrm{d}y\wedge\mathrm{d}x^3
  =\frac{2\kappa}{f(y)^2}\,
       \tilde e^{\hat 2}\wedge\tilde e^{\hat 3}.
\]
It gives
\[
  \tilde R=\frac{4\kappa}{f(y)^2}
  =\frac{4\kappa b_0^2e^{2\kappa y^2}}{\ell_*^4},
\]
in agreement with the scalar curvature above. This is the
Levi--Civita connection determined by the chosen toy metric, not a
derivation of the independent gauge connection or its dynamics from
the matrix action.

For comparison, assume in addition that the continuum trace uses
the symplectic density
\[
  \rho(y)=\frac{1}{|\operatorname{Pf}\Theta|}
         =\frac{1}{|a|b(y)}
\]
and that the scalar kinetic term is built from the two-commutator
tensor \(K_\Theta^{\mu\nu}\). These are extra assumptions for
nonconstant \(\Theta\); neither the trace map nor this kinetic term
is derived here. The corresponding divergence operator is
\[
  \mathcal L_\Theta\phi
  :=\rho^{-1}\partial_\mu
       \bigl(\rho K_\Theta^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu\phi\bigr)
  =a^2(\partial_0^2-\partial_1^2)\phi
    +b(y)^2(\partial_y^2+\partial_3^2)\phi
    +2\kappa y\,b(y)^2\partial_y\phi .
\]
Define instead a conformally related metric \(g_\rho\) by
\[
  g_\rho^{\mu\nu}:=\rho K_\Theta^{\mu\nu},
  \qquad
  (g_\rho)_{\mu\nu}
  =\operatorname{diag}\!\left(
      \frac{b}{|a|},-\frac{b}{|a|},
      \frac{|a|}{b},\frac{|a|}{b}\right).
\]
It is Lorentzian and \(\sqrt{|\det g_\rho|}=1\), whence
\[
  \Box_{g_\rho}
  =\partial_\mu\bigl(\rho K_\Theta^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu\bigr),
  \qquad
  \mathcal L_\Theta=\rho^{-1}\Box_{g_\rho}.
\]
Thus the Poisson-trace operator is a position-dependent scalar
multiple of this metric's Laplace--Beltrami operator, not exactly
the Laplace--Beltrami operator itself. Since \(\rho\ne0\), the two
operators have the same homogeneous zero-mode equation, but are
different differential operators and need not have the same
spectrum or heat kernel.

The metric \(g_\rho\) is conformal to the hand-chosen
\(\tilde g\):
\[
  g_\rho^{\mu\nu}
  =\ell_*^4\rho\,\tilde g^{\mu\nu}.
\]
Its scalar curvature, in the same curvature convention as above, is
\[
  R[g_\rho]
  =-\frac{b(y)}{|a|}
       \bigl(2\kappa+6\kappa^2y^2\bigr),
\]
which differs in general from \(\tilde R\). Accordingly, \(\tilde R\)
is not the curvature associated with this assumed
Poisson-trace operator through \(g_\rho\). Neither curvature is
derived from the matrix action by this comparison.
This is only an algebraic construction: \(b(y)\), \(\kappa\), and
the metric identification are chosen by hand. Accordingly,
\(\tilde R\) is a property of those choices, not a prediction of the
matrix action or the emergent curvature
\(R[g]\) of Chapter~\ref{ch:vierbein-emergence}, and not the
heat-kernel curvature contribution of Chapter~\ref{ch:heat-kernel}.
A metric identification does not by itself supply a matrix-derived
coframe, a dynamical connection, or a local Lorentz gauge symmetry.
The coframe above is chosen rather than matrix-derived, and its
Lorentz-related freedom is not derived from the matrix action. The matrix-derived criterion
of Problem~D remains open. Problems~B, C, D, F and~G remain open.
\end{remark}

\section{Exercises}
\label{sec:ch3-exercises}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Dimensions.}
    Assign mass dimensions to \(\hat X^\mu\), \(\Theta^{\mu\nu}\),
    \(\Phi\), \(v\), \(\lambda\), and \(g_{\mathrm{YM}}\) in four
    dimensions, with \(\hbar=c=1\). Confirm~\eqref{eq:LUV-def}.
  \item \textbf{Vacuum of the potential.}
    Show that~\eqref{eq:V} is bounded below and that its minima
    satisfy~\eqref{eq:vacuum-manifold}. Linearize
    \(\Phi=v\,\mathbb{I}+\sigma+i\pi\) in the internal space
    (ignoring tangent structure) and read off \(m_\sigma^2\) and
    \(m_\pi^2\).
  \item \textbf{Why \(N\ge 4\).}
    If the internal dimension is \(N=2\), can a vacuum of the
    form~\eqref{eq:VEV} exist? What infrared representation content
    is then missing?
  \item \textbf{Commutator to derivative.}
    In the Moyal plane with constant \(\Theta^{12}=-\Theta^{21}=\theta\)
    and all other components zero, verify the leading term
    of~\eqref{eq:dictionary} on a plane-wave matrix
    \(\Phi=e^{ik\cdot\hat X}\). Where do higher powers of \(\theta\)
    appear?
  \item \textbf{IKKT vacuum energy.}
    Evaluate the first term of~\eqref{eq:Smatrix} on the
    \(c\)-number background~\eqref{eq:NC}. Show that it is a
    constant proportional to \(\mathrm{Tr}(\Theta_{\mu\nu}\Theta^{\mu\nu})\).
    This constant is a preview of the \(c_0\LUV^4\) problem.
  \item \textbf{Transformation law.}
    Starting from~\eqref{eq:Phi-transform} and the representative
    vacuum~\eqref{eq:VEV}, compute the infinitesimal variation of
    \(\Phi\) under a simultaneous internal rotation and local Lorentz
    transformation that preserve the vacuum. Which generators are
    broken? (This is the algebra of Chapter~10, prepared here.)
  \item \textbf{No graviton in the scalar truncation.}
    Set \(N=1\) and drop the tangent index, reducing \(\Phi\) to a
    complex scalar. Show that the fluctuation spectrum of
    \eqref{eq:Smicro} contains no spin-2 state. This is the
    Chapter~1 ceiling written as a homework problem.
\end{enumerate}
