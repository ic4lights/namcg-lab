\chapter[Heat kernel and $\Geff$]{Heat Kernel and the Coefficient Map to \(\Geff\)}
\label{ch:heat-kernel}

\paragraph{Learning outcomes.}
After this chapter the reader should be able to:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item write the one-loop effective action of condensate
        fluctuations as a proper-time integral of the heat kernel;
  \item distinguish the universal \(R\) term in \(a_1\) from
        curvature-linear \(a_2\) terms generated by dimensionful
        endomorphisms, and identify the coefficient of the
        cosmological term;
  \item state the conditional, truncated heat-kernel identity and
        identify the operator, determinant-weight, threshold, and
        scheme data needed to evaluate the physical \(R\)-coefficient;
  \item state what is computed, what is scheme-dependent, and what
        is left for Chapter~12;
  \item enter Chapter~9 knowing why a second-order heat kernel is
        not a license for fourth-order kinetic terms.
\end{enumerate}

Theorem~\ref{thm:mT} gave a massless tensor kinetic operator. This
chapter records the conditional heat-kernel identity that can
contribute to its prefactor on the \emph{emergent} background; the
physical coefficient requires the sector calculation of Problem~F.

\section{One-loop effective action on the emergent geometry}
\label{sec:ch8-W}

For the sectors actually included in a gauge-fixed calculation, let
\(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sec}}\) be their index set, \(w_s\) their determinant weights
relative to a real bosonic determinant, and \(\mathcal O_s\) their
operators. Define
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ch8-sector-sum}
  W_E^{(1)}
  =\frac12\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sec}}}w_s
      \operatorname{Tr}_{V_s}\ln\mathcal O_s,
  \qquad
  \mathcal N_0
  :=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sec}}}w_s
      \operatorname{tr}_{V_s}\mathbb I_s .
\end{equation}
The formulae below use a truncated, unresummed small-\(s\)
heat-kernel expansion. A common pair of proper-time limits applies
only to sectors explicitly assigned those limits; otherwise limits
and thresholds remain sector-dependent. If \(E_s\) contains a mass
term, resumming its exponential yields sector-dependent threshold
functions rather than the polynomial cutoff terms below.

After condensation, split
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:split-loop}
  \Phi=\langle\Phi\rangle+\eta,
\end{equation}
where \(\eta\) contains every algebraic block of Chapter~5. Integrate
\(\eta\) at one loop on a slowly varying emergent background
\(g_{\mu\nu}\), using Presentation~B. The dictionary is needed to
attribute this calculation to Presentation~A, not to formulate it in
Presentation~B. For each specified sector the fluctuation operator is
of Laplace type,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:O-eta}
  \mathcal{O}_\eta
  =-g^{\mu\nu}D_\mu D_\nu+E[\langle\Phi\rangle,g],
\end{equation}
with a bundle endomorphism \(E\) that includes the second derivative
of \(V\) and curvature couplings generated by the kinetic term.

The one-loop effective action is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:W-again}
  W_E^{(1)}
  =\frac12\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sec}}}w_s
      \operatorname{Tr}_{V_s}\ln\mathcal O_s
  =-\frac12\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sec}}}w_s
    \int_{s_{\min,s}}^{s_{\max,s}}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}\,
    \operatorname{Tr}_{V_s}e^{-s\mathcal O_s},
\end{equation}
after Wick rotation to a Euclidean section. For a sector explicitly
assigned common limits, these are
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:s-limits}
  s_{\min}=\LUV^{-2},\qquad
  s_{\max}=\mu^{-2},
\end{equation}
where \(\LUV\) is the kinematic cutoff~\eqref{eq:LUV-def}. Other
sectors may have distinct \(s_{\min,s}={\LUV}_s^{-2}\) and
\(s_{\max,s}=\mu_s^{-2}\); no common infrared scale is presumed.

\begin{assumption}[Laplace-type infrared operator]
  \label{ass:laplace}
  After Theorem~\ref{thm:mT} and Assumption~\ref{ass:gap-psi}, the
  tensor block of \(\mathcal{O}_\eta\) is second order and of Laplace
  type. Higher-derivative pieces are treated as perturbations and
  reserved for Chapter~9.
\end{assumption}

\section{Seeley--DeWitt expansion, used not re-derived}
\label{sec:ch8-SDW}

The heat-kernel diagonal on a four-dimensional Riemannian section
admits
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:SDW-again}
  \mathrm{tr}_V\langle x|e^{-s\mathcal{O}_\eta}|x\rangle
  \sim
  \frac{1}{(4\pi s)^{2}}
  \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
  s^k a_k(x),
\end{equation}
where \(\mathrm{tr}_V\) is the fibre trace on the bundle in which
\(\eta\) takes values. The first two scalar densities are
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:a0a1}
  \begin{aligned}
    a_0&=\mathrm{tr}_V\mathbb{I},\\
    a_1&=\mathrm{tr}_V\Bigl(\tfrac16 R-E\Bigr).
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The coefficient \(a_2\) contains \(R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\),
\(R_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\nu}\), \(R^2\), \(E^2\), \(RE\), and derivative
terms. It multiplies a logarithm and is needed in Chapter~9. Its
curvature-linear terms can also contribute to the Einstein--Hilbert
coefficient when dimensionful endomorphism insertions are present,
so they cannot be omitted from the full Problem~F calculation.

Insert the sector heat-kernel expansions into~\eqref{eq:W-again} and
integrate term by term, using each sector's assigned limits:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:W-expand}
  \begin{aligned}
      W_E^{(1)}
    &\supset
      \sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sec}}}w_s
      \int\mathrm{d}^4x\,\sqrt{g}\,
      \Biggl[
        -\frac{a_{0,s}}{64\pi^2}
          \bigl({\LUV}_s^4-\mu_s^4\bigr)
        -\frac{a_{1,s}}{32\pi^2}
          \bigl({\LUV}_s^2-\mu_s^2\bigr)\\
      &\qquad\qquad
        -\frac{a_{2,s}}{32\pi^2}
          \ln\Bigl(\frac{{\LUV}_s^2}{\mu_s^2}\Bigr)
        +\cdots
      \Biggr].
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The \(a_k\) are the traced coefficients on \(V_s\). These are
termwise integrals of the truncated, unresummed expansion, not exact
massive-sector threshold integrals. Wick rotation and the
Euclidean-to-Lorentzian sign are applied only after the weighted
sector sum is specified.

\section{Evaluating \(a_1\) on the condensate}
\label{sec:ch8-a1}

The universal \(a_1\) term and its endomorphism contribution can
produce curvature terms. In addition, a dimensionful part of the
endomorphism can generate a curvature-linear term in \(a_2\).

\paragraph{Universal piece.}
The \(\tfrac16 R_E\,\mathrm{tr}_{V_s}\mathbb{I}_s\) contribution is
present for every specified Laplace-type sector. Its universal
truncated \(a_1\) term alone supplies
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:universal-R}
  W_E^{(1)}
  \supset
  -\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sec}}}
    \frac{w_s\operatorname{tr}_{V_s}\mathbb I_s}{192\pi^2}
    \bigl({\LUV}_s^2-\mu_s^2\bigr)
    \int\mathrm{d}^4x\,\sqrt{g}\,R_E
\end{equation}
This omits the endomorphism contribution and does not fix the total
Einstein--Hilbert coefficient. Resummed masses instead produce
sector-dependent threshold functions.

\paragraph{Endomorphism and threshold terms.}
For a specified sector, write \(E_s=E_{0,s}+Q_sR_E+\cdots\).
Its contribution to the coefficient of \(R_E\) in \(a_1\) is
\(\operatorname{tr}_{V_s}(\tfrac16\mathbb I_s-Q_s)\), not a number
fixed by the fibre dimension alone. Massive sectors require the
threshold treatment specified for their operators.

\begin{theorem}[Coefficient map]
  \label{thm:Geff}
  For specified gauge-fixed operators, determinant weights,
  per-sector proper-time limits, and finite-counterterm scheme
  \(\mathcal S\), the truncated, unresummed expansion gives the
  Euclidean curvature coefficient
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:Geff-thm}
    C_R^E
    =-\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sec}}}
       \frac{w_s\mathcal A_{1,R,s}}{32\pi^2}
       ({\LUV}_s^2-\mu_s^2)
     -\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sec}}}
       \frac{w_s\mathcal A_{2,R,s}}{32\pi^2}
       \ln\Bigl(\frac{{\LUV}_s^2}{\mu_s^2}\Bigr)
     +c_R(\mathcal S),
  \end{equation}
  where \(\mathcal A_{k,R,s}\) is the coefficient of \(R_E\) in
  the traced \(a_{k,s}\), \(\mathcal A_{1,R,s}\) includes the
  specified endomorphism, and \(c_R(\mathcal S)\) is the local
  curvature counterterm in that scheme. Resummed mass terms require
  sector-dependent threshold functions instead. After continuation,
  define the Lorentzian matching by
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:W-EH}
    W_L\supset\frac{1}{16\pi G_{\mathrm{eff}}}
      \int\mathrm{d}^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,R_L,
  \end{equation}
  but this identity supplies no physical value or sign until the
  sector data, thresholds, counterterms, and continuation are
  specified and evaluated.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Conditional parameterization]
  The working parameterization may be retained as a conditional
  notation, not as a computed result:
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:Geff-structure}
    \frac{1}{\Geff}
    =A\,\mathcal N_0\,\LUV^2
     +B\,v^2\ln\Bigl(\frac{\LUV}{\mu}\Bigr)
     +C\,\mu^2,
  \end{equation}
  where \(\mathcal N_0\) is defined in~\eqref{eq:ch8-sector-sum}.
  No factor of \(1/6\) is absorbed into it. The coefficients
  \(A,B,C\) are undetermined; distinct sector thresholds need not
  share \(\mu\). Problem~F remains open.
\end{remark}

\section{The quartic term}
\label{sec:ch8-quartic}

Under the continuation and Lorentzian vacuum-action convention of
Appendix~\ref{app:seeley-dewitt}, the same truncated, unresummed
expansion produces
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:W-Lambda}
  W_L\supset-\rho_\Lambda\int\mathrm{d}^4x\,\sqrt{-g},
  \qquad
  \rho_\Lambda
  =-\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sec}}}
    \frac{w_s\operatorname{tr}_{V_s}\mathbb I_s}{64\pi^2}
    \bigl({\LUV}_s^4-\mu_s^4\bigr)+\cdots.
\end{equation}
This sum uses the same sectors and determinant weights as
Appendix~\ref{app:seeley-dewitt}. It reduces to
\(-\mathcal N_0(\LUV^4-\mu^4)/(64\pi^2)\) only when all included
sectors are assigned the same limits.
Identifying this with a cosmological term,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:Lambda-naive}
  \frac{\Lambda_{\mathrm{naive}}}{8\pi\Geff}=\rho_\Lambda,
\end{equation}
would give \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{naive}}\) of order \(\LUV^2\) only
conditionally: the weighted quartic contribution must be nonzero
and uncancelled, and a physical relation between \(\Geff\) and
\(\LUV\) must be specified. Finite local vacuum terms and
counterterms must also be included in that assessment. Without
these inputs, this equation supplies no scale estimate for
\(\Lambda_{\mathrm{naive}}\).

This chapter does not cancel it. It isolates it. Two remarks prevent
the isolation from being mistaken for a solution.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A bare counterterm \(\int\sqrt{-g}\,\Lambda_{\mathrm{ct}}\)
        can subtract \(\rho_\Lambda\) at one scale. It cannot explain
        why the remainder after subtraction is \(10^{-120}\) in
        Planck units. Subtraction without a principle is fine tuning.
  \item Chapter~12 assigns the principle to a boundary-entropy
        functional (HBEV). Until that chapter, \(\rho_\Lambda\) is a
        debt.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Which background the heat kernel sees}
\label{sec:ch8-which-g}

Assumption~\ref{ass:aux-metric} declared \(g^{(0)}\) non-dynamical.
The heat kernel of this chapter is nevertheless written on
\(g_{\mu\nu}\). The justification is the derivative expansion:

\begin{proposition}[Scaffold replacement]
  \label{prop:scaffold}
  Let \(g_{\mu\nu}=g^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}\) with \(h\) slowly
  varying on the scale \(\LUV^{-1}\). Replacing \(g^{(0)}\) by \(g\)
  inside \(\mathcal{O}_\eta\) changes \(W\) by operators of the form
  \(\LUV^{2}\|h-h^{(0)}\|\), \(\|h-h^{(0)}\|R\), and higher. On the
  Minkowski scaffold \(g^{(0)}=\eta\) these are the same operators
  already kept in~\eqref{eq:W-expand}. Self-consistently substituting
  the emergent metric is therefore valid through the order of this
  conditional truncated identity.
\end{proposition}

At higher order the replacement produces the backreaction tensor
\(\mathcal{G}_{\mu\nu}\) of~\eqref{eq:SW}. That tensor is not needed
to define the conditional coefficient. It is needed to define the extra stress of
Chapters~11--14.

\section{Numerical size}
\label{sec:ch8-size}

The physical size of \(\Geff\) and the relation between \(\LUV\) and
the Planck scale are undetermined until Problem~F specifies and
evaluates the sector operators, determinant weights, thresholds,
continuation, and counterterm scheme.
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:Geff-size}
  \Geff\quad\text{is not numerically determined by the present data.}
\end{equation}

\section{Exercises}
\label{sec:ch8-exercises}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Proper-time integrals.}
    For one specified sector with assigned limits, perform the
    term-by-term integrals producing~\eqref{eq:W-expand} from
    \eqref{eq:SDW-again} and~\eqref{eq:W-again}, treating \(a_k\)
    as \(s\)-independent. State why this is not a resummed massive
    threshold calculation.
  \item \textbf{Dimensions.}
    Confirm that \(a_0\LUV^4\), \(a_1\LUV^2\), and
    \(a_2\ln(\LUV/\mu)\) all have mass dimension four.
  \item \textbf{Conformal value of \(\xi\).}
    For a scalar with non-minimal term \(\xi R\phi^2\), show that
    \(\xi=1/6\) cancels its curvature contribution to \(a_1\).
    Explain why this does not determine the full locked-block result.
  \item \textbf{Mass thresholds.}
    Compare the truncated polynomial terms with the integrals that
    retain \(\exp(-s m^2)\). Explain why distinct masses can require
    distinct threshold functions and limits.
  \item \textbf{Conditional curvature coefficient.}
    Given sector operators, weights, limits, and counterterms, derive
    the Euclidean \(R\)-coefficient from supplied \(a_1,a_2\), and
    identify the continuation data needed for Lorentzian matching.
  \item \textbf{Sector weights.}
    For a supplied gauge-fixed sector list, determine the determinant
    weights and compute \(\mathcal N_0\). Explain why this alone does
    not determine the curvature coefficient.
  \item \textbf{Scaffold error.}
    Expand Proposition~\ref{prop:scaffold} to first order in
    \(h_{\mu\nu}\) on a flat scaffold and list the operators
    produced. Which of them renormalize \(\Geff\), and which
    renormalize higher-curvature couplings?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Bridge to Chapter~9}
\label{sec:ch8-bridge}

Once the sector calculation is completed, the action through two
derivatives has the form
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:W-through-2}
  W[g]
  =\int\mathrm{d}^4x\,\sqrt{-g}
   \Bigl(
     -\rho_\Lambda
     +\frac{1}{16\pi\Geff}R
     +\alpha\,a_2[g]
     +\cdots
   \Bigr).
\end{equation}
The \(a_2\) density contains \(R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\)
and \(R_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\nu}\). If those operators are kept as
propagating fourth-order kinetic terms for \(h_{\mu\nu}\),
Ostrogradsky ghosts appear. Chapter~9 classifies the dangerous
operators, states the conditions under which they may be treated as
non-propagating corrections, and introduces the Einsteinian cubic
completion used later to regularize curvature singularities without
reopening the ghost problem.
