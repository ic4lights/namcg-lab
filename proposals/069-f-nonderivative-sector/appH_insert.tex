\paragraph{Conjugate-coordinate class times a fuzzy factor: the Hessian of a tree stabiliser on \(K\), the one-loop weights on the cubic branch and the exchange of the scale of \(K\) (conditional; bounded no-go; correction of the cubic and mass branches of the preceding paragraph).}
This paragraph supplements the preceding one, in which a tree cubic term and a tree mass term on \(K\) were added to the tree term \(V_{\mathrm t}\) while \(U_4\), \(U_2\) and \(Y\) were those of the operators \(H_0+\Sigma^{AB}\mathbb F_{AB}\) of the paragraph on product backgrounds. Keep its setting and notation, with \(K=S^2_N\), \(X^i=\chi J^i\) in three of the six directions, and take the cubic term of Equation~\eqref{eq:Smatrix} on those three directions with \(C_{ijk}=\mathrm i\,c\,\epsilon_{ijk}/g^2\), \(c\) real, so that the \(K\) part of the tree action is \(g^{-2}\bigl[-\tfrac14\operatorname{tr}[X^i,X^j]^2+\tfrac{\mathrm ic}{3}\epsilon_{ijk}\operatorname{tr}X^iX^jX^k\bigr]\), which is real for Hermitian matrices (for real \(C_{ijk}\) the cubic trace is imaginary). Definition~\ref{def:matrix-coords-ext} does not extend the cubic term to the transverse coordinates, so this term is a supplied input, as in the preceding paragraph. Write \(r_c=c/2\chi\) and \(\kappa=2-c/\chi=2(1-r_c)\).

\emph{Hessian.} On \(S^2_N\) the tree term is \(V_{\mathrm t}=\bar a(\chi^4-\tfrac23c\chi^3)\), stationary at \(\chi=c/2\) with \(\partial^2V_{\mathrm t}/\partial(\ln\chi)^2=4\bar a\chi^4>0\) there; this is the fuzzy-sphere solution of the matrix model with a cubic term~\cite{IsoKimuraTanakaWakatsuki2001}. The second variation of the cubic term is \(\mathrm ic\,\epsilon_{ijk}\operatorname{tr}(a^ia^jX^k)\): it acts on the three components of the vector along \(K\) only and is linear in the adjoint action of \(X^k\). With \(L^2\) the adjoint Casimir and \(S\) the spin-one generators on the index \(i\), the operator of those three components in the background Feynman gauge is exactly
\[
  H_V^K=\chi^2\bigl(L^2-\kappa\,S\cdot L\bigr),\qquad \kappa=2-\frac c\chi ,
\]
with eigenvalues \(\chi^2(l(l+1)-\kappa l)\), \(\chi^2(l(l+1)+\kappa)\), \(\chi^2(l(l+1)+\kappa(l+1))\) and multiplicities \(2l+3\), \(2l+1\), \(2l-1\) (Hessian of the action built from the matrices at \(N\le5\), six pairs \((\chi,c)\), residual below \(10^{-13}\)); \(\kappa=2\) is the spectrum of the preceding paragraph. The ghost operator, the spinor operator and the other seven components of the vector are unchanged, since the cubic term contains neither ghosts nor spinors. There is no negative mode for \(-1\le\kappa\le2\), that is \(0\le c\le3\chi\); at \(\kappa=-2\) the mode \(l=1\), \(j=0\) has the eigenvalue \(-2\chi^2\). At \(\chi=c/2\) the operator is \(\chi^2L^2\) on the three components, the index part \(v^K\) vanishes identically, and the Hessian without gauge fixing annihilates the gauge directions, which it does not at \(c=0\). A tree mass term \(\tfrac12\mu^2\operatorname{tr}X^iX_i\) on \(K\) adds \(\mu^2\) to the same three components and to nothing else.

\emph{Weights with the Hessian.} The factorisation of the paragraph on product backgrounds is an identity for commuting operators of the two factors and holds with \(v^K\) the index part of \(H_V^K\). Its statement that the index parts of the vector and of the spinors agree at second order in the flux does not: with weights \(w\) and eigenvalues \(\lambda\) of \(v^K-\tau^K\), \(\sum w=\sum w\lambda=0\) and, exactly,
\[
  \sum w\lambda^2=(\kappa^2-4)\,\chi^4N^2(N^2-1)=(c^2\chi^2-4c\chi^3)\,N^2(N^2-1),
\]
so \(s_0^M(v^K-\tau^K)\) starts at second order in the flux of \(K\). Then \(\hat U\) of the preceding paragraph does not converge at small proper time. With a lower limit \(s_\Lambda\) the term \(U_4\) is replaced by \(-(F/32\pi^2)\sqrt{|g|}\,\mathcal U\),
\[
  \mathcal U=\int_{s_\Lambda}^\infty\frac{\mathrm ds}{s^3}\bigl(v^K-\tau^K\bigr)
  =-\tfrac12\sum w\lambda^2\ln\lambda+\tfrac12\Bigl(\sum w\lambda^2\Bigr)\Bigl(\ln\frac1{s_\Lambda}-\gamma_{\mathrm E}+\tfrac32\Bigr)+O(s_\Lambda),
\]
whose logarithm multiplies a term in \(\chi^3\), of the form of the cubic term, and a term in \(\chi^2\), of the form of a mass term on \(K\), which Equation~\eqref{eq:Smatrix} does not contain; both carry \(\sqrt{|g|}\), not \(\rho_M\). So in the Poisson limit on \(M\) the cubic branch is not closed at one loop without a mass counterterm, and the scaling argument of the preceding paragraph (dependence on \(\chi^2/\lambda\) alone) does not apply at fixed \(c\). The regulated \(\mathcal U\) is of order \(N^4\), against \(N^2\) at \(c=0\), and is negative for \(s_\Lambda\chi^2N^2\le0.9\), a limit above the spectrum of \(K\), for the sampled \(-1<\kappa<2\) (it vanishes at \(s_\Lambda\chi^2N^2=0.926\), \(N=512\)); there \(V_0>0\) and \(q_b^2<0\) in the vacuum term of the preceding paragraph, the sign for which that paragraph finds a Yukawa pole, but the value depends on the regulator. The weights with two derivatives stay finite: \(T\) is unchanged, and \(\hat V/\hat T\to\kappa^2/4\) (exact spectra, \(N\le512\)), so that at large \(N\)
\[
  \frac VT=(1-r_c)^2,\qquad W_R=V-T=-T\,r_c(2-r_c),
\]
with \(V=0\) and \(W_R=-T\) exactly at \(r_c=1\). There, in the units of the paragraph on product backgrounds and at equal weights, \(c_T=\tfrac16W_R-4W_X=\tfrac13T>0\), and \(\tfrac16W_R-2W_X=\tfrac1{12}T\) in the presentation \(r=-2\) of that paragraph; the forms of the paragraph on fibre-point forms, which take \(V>0\), degenerate, since \(A+B=\tfrac18V\to0\), \(\varrho=(V+2T)/3V\to\infty\) and \(c^{\mathrm{TT}}\to0\). The condition \(V=T\) holds only at \(r_c=0\) and at \(r_c=2\), where the negative mode is present. For the mass term \(\sum w\lambda=3\mu^2N^2\ne0\), so \(W_R\) itself is logarithmic at small proper time.

\emph{Correction.} The three forms of \(\beta_K\) of the preceding paragraph with a tree cubic or a tree mass term, and its example at \(x=\tfrac3{10}\), use \(U_4\), \(U_2\) and \(Y\) of the operators without the Hessian of that term, a restriction which its scope sentence does not state; they are the one-loop forms only as \(c/\chi\to0\) and \(\mu\to0\). The example has \(r_c=1.168\), \(\kappa=-0.336\), so with the Hessian \(V/T=0.028\) and \(\varrho=23.9\) there, and the term that replaces \(U_4=-0.09\) is, in the units of that example and for \(s_\Lambda\chi^2N^2=\tfrac12\), \(27.5\) at \(N=16\) and \(441\) at \(N=64\), against the tree quartic \(7.935\). The example is therefore not a one-loop stationary point of the action with that cubic term, and the statements of the preceding paragraph that \(\beta_K=1\) is attained at a local minimum with \(\hat U>0\) with a tree cubic term, and that \(V=T\) is approached at large \(N\) on \(S^2_N\) without a tuned \(K\), hold only for the operators at \(c=\mu=0\). The relation \(\beta_K=-2\,\partial_{\ln\chi}(\rho_MV_{\mathrm t}/g^2)/U_{\chi\chi}\) does not use the form of the loop term and stands, with \(U_2\) now containing \(W_R\) at leading order.

\emph{Tree curve and the ratio of the one-loop to the tree terms.} With \(\tilde U=\rho_M\bar a\chi^4/g^2\), \(L'\) the derivative of the one-loop density with respect to \(\ln\chi\) and \(\delta\) its second derivative divided by \(4\tilde U\), stationarity and the relation above give, for the quartic and cubic terms,
\[
  1-r_c=-\frac{L'}{4\tilde U},\qquad \beta_K=\frac{2(r_c-1)}{4-3r_c+\delta},\qquad \frac VT=\Bigl(\frac{L'}{4\tilde U}\Bigr)^2 ,
\]
the last at large \(N\). So on this branch \(V=T\) requires a one-loop slope equal in size to the slope of the tree quartic, and \(\beta_K=1\) requires \(r_c=(6+\delta)/5\), where \(V/T=(1+\delta)^2/25\); at \(r_c=2\), \(\beta_K=2/(\delta-2)\). Where the one-loop term is small against the tree term the minimum is at \(r_c\to1\), which, unlike the quartic term alone, exists at tree level; there \(\beta_K\to0\), the case (S2), and \(V/T\to0\). Within one loop, on \(S^2_N\) with this cubic term, no point has \(\beta_K=1\), \(V=T\) and no negative mode. At the example of the preceding paragraph, with its operators, the ratios of the one-loop to the tree contributions are \(-1\) for the slope (by stationarity; the one-loop slope is \(0.168\) of the slope of the quartic), \(-5.04/15.72=-0.32\) for \(U_{\chi\chi}\), and the vacuum term has no tree part among the computed forms: on helicity \(\pm2\) at level one its fibre average is \(\tfrac1{15}V_0m^{-6}(m^2q_0^2+\ell^2k^2)\,c\cdot c\), which gives terms of the kind \(V_{00}\), \(V_{01}\) of the paragraph on fibre-point forms as fixed moments of \(V_0\) (normalisations not compared). These are values at one loop; where the ratios are of order one they are not controlled, and higher loops are not computed.

\emph{Exchange of the scale of \(K\).} For a real wave with \(\delta\ln\sqrt{|g|}=\sigma_1=-t/2m^2\) at first order, as in the static channel of the preceding paragraph, the terms without derivatives are \(\tfrac12U_{\chi\chi}(\delta\ln\chi)^2+L'\,\delta\ln\chi\,\sigma_1+U_4\sigma_1^2\) plus the second-order parts of \(U_2\) and of the first term. Eliminating \(\delta\ln\chi\) for \(q^2\) below \(m_\chi^2\) gives \((U_4-\tfrac14\beta_KL')\sigma_1^2\), so the term \(q_*^2\) of the preceding paragraph contains
\[
  q_*^2\big|_\chi=-\frac{\beta_KL'L}{8m^4}=\frac{\beta_KL'}{4U_4}\,q_b^2 ,
\]
which is non-positive at a local minimum, since \(\beta_KL'=2L'^2/U_{\chi\chi}\). At the example of the preceding paragraph, with its operators, it is \(-14.8\,q_b^2\), so there the exchange of \(\chi\) exceeds the vacuum term and has the tachyonic sign. The second-order parts of \(U_2\) and of the first term, and the constant of the first-term logarithm, are not computed, so the sign of \(q_*^2-q_b^2\) is not determined.

These statements are for the hypotheses of the preceding paragraph, \(K=S^2_N\) with fixed \(\Lambda^i=J^i\), the cubic term with \(C_{ijk}\propto\mathrm i\epsilon_{ijk}\) on the three directions of \(K\) and no fermion bilinear, the background Feynman gauge away from \(\chi=c/2\), one loop, the Poisson limit on \(M\) with a lower proper-time limit where one is stated, and large \(N\) for the ratios of weights. The Hessian, the spectra, the moments and the relations on the tree curve are exact for these forms; the limits of the weights and the sign of the regulated \(\mathcal U\) are numerical.
