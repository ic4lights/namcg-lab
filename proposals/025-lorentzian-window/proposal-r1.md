# Proposal 025-lorentzian-window, round 1

## Result
Bounded results; Problem F stays open; no sign of G_eff follows.

1. **Fibre admissibility (R-F2).** The three candidates are three different things. (a) The Poisson-limit fibre is a compact S^2 with unbounded harmonic number (this is what 018-023 call "unbounded fibre"). (b) The exact algebraic bound s<=n of 1910.00839 eq (2.9) has the classical counterpart |L|=ell, a constant (10 points; one of the two su(2) radii vanishes, the other equals ell). (c) The local bound s<=m=r^-1 x^0 of 2502.02498 eq (70) grows with the point, is stated there as a correction to earlier cutoffs, and is not the area bound; I do not reconcile (b) and (c). Admissibility (1910.00839 eq (3.44), footnote 8) has the symbol-level content -sigma(C42)>=0 (a cone), conditional on the footnote-8 identification. With the cone and any fibre bound, a function of the central C42 is a compact window and kappa is not needed (kappa only rescales Lambda^2 by 2kappa-1 at fixed fibre bound). Neither chi nor the base scale is fixed; the Berezin-type multiplier is not computed. The window is built from background M^{AB}, so the regulated trace is not shown to be covariant in the dynamical X^a.
2. **Lorentzian i-epsilon (R-F1/E1).** In a Gaussian Casimir window the trace is finite for every s and the leading-symbol density is closed form. The principal-value mass moment <(sigma-i0)^-1> is positive in 4000 samples (closed form 2/(a+c) for lambda=(-c,a,a,a)) and 1.1-2.3 times the Euclid-contraction value at eight orbit points (same sign); its imaginary part is positive. The Lorentzian centre flux always has one real pair and one imaginary pair; F_abF^ab takes both signs on the leaf; the vector Zeeman block has phases (+-2b) and real exponentials e^(+-2sa); the log det is finite; Euclidean-type growth is absent order by order in the flux and present as an integral-representation property for the electric pair.
3. **Exact invariant sector.** The H^4 x H^3 kernel expansion reproduces the appendix density (5m^2-3 ell^2)/(4m^2) by an independent route, and gives the windowed Lorentzian weight-two function Q(a): Re Q changes sign at a=sinh(tau)~0.62 (not at a^2=3/5) and Im Q->pi; it carries a scale-dependent term -2 ln(mu^2 t) proportional to the mode count (cancels only if N_b=N_f at this order).
4. **Sign of the attractive coupling (G_eff>0): not derived.** Exact hypothesis class in which something is established: Poisson limit, one loop, leading symbol, four-matrix class with mu^2=-3, Gaussian Casimir window, A=0, invariant scalar sector and the mass-type (potential) channel only. In that class the Lorentzian moment is finite and positive. The Einstein-Hilbert coefficient needs the second variation under A of the vector, ghost and fibre-harmonic sectors, which is not done; the order-F^2 term is proportional to F_abF^ab and is indefinite on the leaf; the invariant-sector a_1 is indefinite (zero at m^2=3 ell^2/5); the window-count constant is scale dependent.

## Research and reasoning
**Conventions.** The appendix uses real Hamiltonian vector fields J, so C^here = -C^there (Steinacker, Hermitian adjoint action) at the symbol level. On the invariant sector Box^here = -(Delta_H4 - Delta_H3) (checked symbolically, a1_invariant_sector.py), H_V = -Box, and e^(-is H_V) = e^(-is (Delta_H4 - Delta_H3)). The window e^(-t C41^here) = e^(t Delta_H4).

**Fibre.** S^2 = 2C41-C42 is vertical (023). 1910.00839 eq (3.44): r^-2 Box_H > s^2+s+9/4, with Box_H = -C42+C41 and S^2 = 2s(s+1); in symbols Box_H is horizontal (alpha q_b/2) and S^2 = beta q_f = 2s^2, so the bound is alpha q_b >= beta q_f, that is -sigma(C42)>=0. Footnote 8 of that paper says the admissible modes are precisely those with C2[so(4,2)]>9/2 in its convention and that this is plausible and not investigated, so the cone is conditional. fibre_cutoff.py: the fibre radius as a coadjoint orbit of SU(2)_L equals ell at all 10 points and only one of the two chiral parts is nonzero (consistent with eps M M = 0). So the area bound is constant (the s<=n of the algebra), whereas eq (70) of 2502.02498 is a coherent-state resolution bound growing with x^0; they are different statements and I could not reconcile them from the sources.

**kappa.** -sigma(Delta_kappa) = alpha(kappa-1/2) q_b + beta(1-kappa) q_f. With q_f<=Q fixed the second term is bounded by beta(1-kappa)Q, so for Lambda^2 >> beta Q the window is (alpha/2)(2kappa-1) q_b <= Lambda^2: kappa rescales Lambda^2. This is algebra; it does not fix chi or the base scale.

**Gaussian window.** chi = exp(-sigma_W/Lambda^2): (2pi)^-6 Int d^6xi chi e^(-is sigma) = (4pi)^-3 Lambda^6 det(sigma_W)^(-1/2) prod_k (1+is Lambda^2 lambda_k)^(-1/2), lambda_k the nonzero generalised eigenvalues of (sigma(Box), sigma_W) (rank 4). window_moments.py: the kernel of sigma(Box) is not the vertical plane (relative overlap 0.27-1.0), so no factorisation into a base part and a fibre factor is claimed; the formula needs only the pencil. Mass moment: i Int_0^oo ds prod (1+is lambda_k)^(-1/2), evaluated on the ray s = t e^(-i pi/4) (no singularity in the fourth quadrant). Isotropic closed form from the angular principal value Int_0^pi sin^2 theta dtheta/(a sin^2 theta - c cos^2 theta) = pi/(a+c) (the principal value of Int dt/(a t^2-c) over R vanishes): real part 2/(a+c), matched numerically. Positivity for general a_k is numerical (4000 samples); no proof.

**Flux.** zeeman_lorentz.py: with eta = diag(-1,1,1,1), eta F has eigenvalues (+-a real, +-ib imaginary), a,b>0, at 8 points x 4 directions; tr(F^2) = -F_ab F^ab holds; F_abF^ab/2 = b^2-a^2 is negative for (y^4)^2 <~ 0.4 and positive above. The Zeeman matrix of H_V is i times a Lorentz generator in the vector representation: eigenvalues +-2b (real) and +-2ia (imaginary) (the factor 2 from H_V = -Box - 2sJ Sigma). H_V is eta-self-adjoint, not self-adjoint for the positive inner product, so e^(-isH_V) is eta-unitary and may grow. The factorisation det(sigma+Z) = (sigma^2-4b^2)(sigma^2+4a^2) uses only these eigenvalues at the leading symbol, with sigma and Z commuting; non-commutativity corrections belong to the weight-two terms.

**Invariant sector.** s0_exact_expansion.py: D(z,w) = Int_{H^3} K_4(z;r) K_3(w;d) dvol, cosh r = 1+cosh^2(tau)(cosh d-1); K_3 exact; K_4 = (4 pi z)^-2 e^(-r^2/4z) (r/sinh r)^(3/2) (1+z b_1(r)+...) with b_1 from the transport equation, b_1(0) = -2. The first correction D_1 is a rational function of (z,w); at t=0 (z=-is, w=is) D_1 = is c_s with c_s = 5/4-3/(4a^2) = (5m^2-3 ell^2)/(4m^2), identical to the independent computation of a_1 for the appendix operator (a1_invariant_sector.py: R/6 - omega^2 - div omega) and to the appendix density. An early version used wrong Gaussian moments (gave 7/12-25/(12a^2)); corrected to the weight x^2 e^(-gamma x^2): 3/(2 gamma), 15/(4 gamma^2). s0_weight_two.py: Q(a) = Int dx/x g d_1 with the x->0 subtraction, cross-checked by real-axis integration (agreement 3e-3 at a=0.5, better elsewhere). Re Q = -0.796, -0.326, 0.460, 1.114, 2.405, 3.446, 6.129, 8.884 at a = 0.3, 0.5, 0.775, 1, 1.5, 2, 4, 8; root a = 0.6174; Im Q -> pi.

**Literature.** 1910.00839 and 2502.02498 equations were read in the PDFs as cited. That a smoothing multiplier of the equivariant quantisation map is a function of the Casimirs is an equivariance expectation, not computed. Not verified: 2405.09804 eqs 4.1-4.3 and 2411.02598 eqs 3.4-3.7.

**Hypotheses of the no-go and the forks.** (H1) Poisson limit; (H2) leading symbol plus the invariant-sector weight-two term at A=0; (H3) four-matrix class, mu^2 = -3, bosonic; (H4) Gaussian window of the Casimir pencil; (H5) background-built window. H1 (Moyal): not explored. H2: the second variation under A needs the Weyl calculus on the six-dimensional leaf with A-dependent Hamiltonians; the structure found is a local coefficient times moments; not done. H3 fermions/SUSY: the mode-count term is proportional to N_b-N_f, so supertrace weights would remove the ln mu dependence; not computed. Eight-matrix Hessian: the (5,1) case without a centre variable (023) has no flux; not examined. H4: a sharp window changes the moments; the Gaussian makes them closed form. H5: a regulator built from X^a only (exact finite-N trace as in the 023 compact check) avoids the covariance gap.

```latex
\paragraph{Conjugate-coordinate class: fibre admissibility and the Lorentzian \(i\epsilon\) window (conditional; bounded no-go).}
Keep the setting and notation of the preceding paragraph (Poisson limit, leaf \(\mathbb{CP}^{1,2}\), \(T^a=M^{a4}\), \(\Box=\eta_{ab}D^aD^b\), \(H_V\) with \(\mu^2=-3\), \(C_{42}\), \(C_{41}\), \(C_{31}\), \(\sigma(\cdot)\), \(\Delta_\kappa\)); write \(S^2=2C_{41}-C_{42}\), whose symbol \(\beta q_f\) is purely vertical.

\emph{Fibre cutoffs.} In the Poisson limit the fibre of the leaf is a compact two-sphere and its harmonic number, equivalently the centre variable \(\zeta\), is unbounded; this is the sense of ``unbounded fibre'' in the preceding paragraphs. Two bounds on the harmonic number are in the literature. (a) \(S^2=2s(s+1)\) on the sectors \(\mathcal C^s\) with \(s\le n\), from the \((n+1)\)-dimensional representation of the stabiliser \(SU(2)_L\) on the lowest-weight state~\cite{Steinacker2019NoGhost}; the classical counterpart is the radius of the fibre as a coadjoint orbit of \(SU(2)_L\), the self-dual part of \(M^{ab}\) on the \(\eta\)-orthogonal complement of \(y\): at \(10\) orbit points exactly one of the two radii vanishes and the other equals \(\ell\), independent of \(y\). (b) A local bound \(s\le m=r^{-1}x^0\) from the uncertainty of the coherent states, which the same group states to correct earlier statements on the higher-spin cutoff~\cite{MantaSteinacker2025}; it grows with the point and is not the area bound of (a), and the two are not reconciled here. Neither is a sharp cutoff of commuting matrix-built operators.

\emph{Admissible cone.} For admissible modes, the principal series of \(SO(4,1)\), one has \(r^{-2}\Box_H>s^2+s+9/4\) with \(\Box_H=-C_{42}+C_{41}\) in the convention of~\cite{Steinacker2019NoGhost}, equivalent to \(-C_{42}>9/2\) in that convention (stated there as plausible, not proved, for the \(SO(4,2)\) content of \(\operatorname{End}(\mathcal H_n)\)). With \(\Box_H\) horizontal, \(S^2\) vertical and \(\sigma(C_{42})=-\alpha q_b+\beta q_f\) of the preceding paragraph, the semiclassical content of this bound is the cone \(-\sigma(C_{42})\ge0\), that is \(\alpha q_b\ge\beta q_f\); the Casimirs here and there differ by a sign at the symbol level. Conditional on this identification, a cutoff of \(C_{42}\) restricted to the cone together with a bound \(\beta q_f\le Q\) is a compact region, so a function of the central operator \(C_{42}\) regularises once the fibre is bounded. In that case \(\Delta_\kappa\) is not needed: for fixed \(Q\) and \(\LUV^2\to\infty\) the window \(\{-\sigma(\Delta_\kappa)\le\LUV^2\}\) has its vertical term bounded by \(\beta(1-\kappa)Q\), so \(\kappa\) only rescales \(\LUV^2\) by \(2\kappa-1\) at leading order and the divergence of the window volume at the ends of the interval is absent. The cutoff function and the base scale remain external: the quantisation map is \(SO(4,2)\)-equivariant and so acts on each irreducible component by a scalar, but this multiplier is not computed, and neither \(\chi\) nor the base scale is fixed. The window uses \(M^{AB}\), which are background data and not dynamical matrices, so the regulated trace is not shown to be a diffeomorphism-covariant functional of \(X^a\).

\emph{Lorentzian trace in a window.} Define the one-loop sum as \(\sum\ln(\lambda-i\epsilon)=-\int_0^\infty\frac{\mathrm ds}{s}\operatorname{Tr}\bigl[\chi\,e^{-is(H_V-i\epsilon)}\bigr]\) up to a scale-dependent constant, with \(\chi\) a function of the commuting operator \(\Delta_\kappa\); the contour is the proper time of \(H_V\) with the factor \(e^{-\epsilon s}\), and uses no metric. In a window of compact support (or a Gaussian) the number of modes per unit volume is finite, so the trace is finite for every \(s\), and \(\sum\ln(\lambda-i\epsilon)\) is a finite product over the window whether or not the Schwinger integral converges. For \(\chi=e^{-\sigma_W/\LUV^2}\), \(\sigma_W=-\sigma(\Delta_\kappa)\), the leading-symbol density is the Gaussian \((4\pi)^{-3}\LUV^6\det(\sigma_W)^{-1/2}\prod_{k=1}^{4}(1+is\LUV^2\lambda_k)^{-1/2}\), with \(\lambda_k\) the nonzero generalised eigenvalues of \((\sigma(\Box),\sigma_W)\); the kernel of \(\sigma(\Box)\) is not the vertical plane (checked at eight points). The mass-type moment is \(\langle(\sigma-i0)^{-1}\rangle=i\int_0^\infty\mathrm ds\prod_k(1+is\lambda_k)^{-1/2}\), whose real part is the principal value. For \(\lambda=(-c,a,a,a)\) the real part is \(2/(a+c)\) (closed form, checked), and for \(4000\) samples with \(c\in[0.1,10]\), \(a_k\in[10^{-3},10^{3}]\) it is positive. At eight orbit points (\(\kappa=\tfrac34\), \((y^4)^2\) from \(0.08\) to \(144\)) it is \(1.1\) to \(2.3\) times the value for the Euclidean contraction \(\eta\to\delta\), with the same sign, and the imaginary part is positive.

\emph{Flux.} At the same points the Lorentzian centre flux \(F_{ab}(\zeta)\) has \(\eta F\) with one real pair \(\pm a\) and one imaginary pair \(\pm ib\), \(a,b>0\) at all sampled \(\zeta\), and \(F_{ab}F^{ab}\) takes both signs (negative for \((y^4)^2\lesssim0.4\), positive above). The vector block therefore has Zeeman eigenvalues \(\pm2b\) (phases in \(e^{-is(H_V-i\epsilon)}\)) and \(\pm2ia\) (a factor \(e^{\pm2sa}\)). The Schwinger integrand of the vector sector then grows for the electric pair, bounded by \(e^{2sa}\) with \(a\) linear in the cutoff, while \(\ln\det(\sigma+Z)=\ln(\sigma^2-4b^2)+\ln(\sigma^2+4a^2)\) is finite. The exponential growth of the Euclidean contraction is absent order by order in the flux (each order is a polynomial in \(s\) times \(e^{-is\sigma}\)) and for the magnetic pair, and is present for the electric pair as a property of the integral representation and not of the determinant. The order-\(F^2\) term is proportional to \(F_{ab}F^{ab}\), so its sign is not definite on the leaf.

\emph{Invariant sector, exactly.} On fibre-constant functions \(\Box=-(\Delta_{H^4}-\Delta_{H^3})\) with \(H^4=\mathbb R_\tau\times H^3\), metric \(\mathrm d\tau^2+\cosh^2\tau\,\mathrm dH_3^2\), \(y^4=\ell\sinh\tau\), and the diagonal of \(e^{z\Delta_{H^4}+w\Delta_{H^3}}\) is the \(H^3\) integral of the product of the two heat kernels. Its expansion gives \(D_0=(4\pi)^{-2}z^{-1/2}(z+w\cosh^2\tau)^{-3/2}\), the Gaussian symbol formula above, and, for the pure \(\Box\) term (window removed), the Seeley--DeWitt coefficient \((5m^2-3\ell^2)/(4m^2)\), \(m=y^4\), the formal density of the preceding paragraphs obtained by an independent route. With the window the weight-two part of \(\sum\ln(\lambda-i\epsilon)\) is \(-(4\pi)^{-2}\LUV^2\,Q(a)\), \(a=\sinh\tau\), plus \(-2\) times a scale-dependent logarithm proportional to the number of modes; \(\operatorname{Re}Q\) changes sign at \(a\simeq0.62\) (not at \(a^2=\tfrac35\), the zero of the coefficient above) and is positive above, and \(\operatorname{Im}Q\to\pi\). This is the Lorentzian density of one scalar sector at the background \(A=0\), with a scale-dependent additive constant; it is not the Einstein--Hilbert coefficient.

\emph{Consequences.} (i) The coefficient carrying \(w\) is the weight-two term, a local Poisson-algebraic coefficient multiplied by window moments; the signature enters through the moments and the window, and in the potential channel the Lorentzian moment is finite and positive in the class sampled. (ii) The mode-count term is proportional to the difference between the numbers of bosonic and fermionic modes in the window, so its logarithmic scale dependence cancels only if that difference vanishes at this order. (iii) The weight-two coefficient as a quadratic form in \(A\) is not computed, the vector, ghost and fibre-harmonic sectors are not computed, and neither the sign nor the size of \(w\), hence no sign of an attractive coupling, follows.

This is a statement for the Poisson limit, one loop, the leading symbol and the invariant sector, the four-matrix class with \(\mu^2=-3\), Gaussian windows and the sampled points; the admissible cone is conditional on the identification stated, and the positivity of the moment is numerical. It does not treat fermions, Moyal terms, the dependence on \(A\), or a window built from dynamical matrices only; it identifies no induced metric with the emergent metric of Chapter~\ref{ch:vierbein-emergence} and, since a Euclidean coefficient is not a Lorentzian one, leaves Assumption~\ref{ass:lorentzian} in place (Problem~E). This changes no ledger label; Problem~F remains open.
```
