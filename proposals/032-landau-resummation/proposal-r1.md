# Proposal 032-landau-resummation, round 1

## Result
Bounded no-go, with an exact positive sub-result, for the background-flux (Landau/nilpotent) resummation as infrared cutoff in the CP^{1,2} four-matrix class (Poisson limit, one loop, leading nilpotent symbol, Lorentzian i-epsilon, 028 content). Nothing closes F or E.

1. **Exact orbital factor (established identity, checked three ways).** Theta(s) = Tr e^{-isH0} per unit horizontal volume = -i f_e f_m /(16 pi^2 sinh(s f_e) sin(s f_m - i0)). Checks: Landau-level sum, KMS identity dlnTheta/ds = -i eta^{ab}G_ab at complex s, small-s limit (`s1_orbital.py`).
2. **Mass-supertrace term is finite and contour-robust (exact closed form, numerically confirmed).** i int ds Theta = (1/16 pi^2)(1/s0 + g), g(a,b) = pi[ b S(b/a) + i a S(a/b) ], S(r) = sum_{n>=1} (-1)^n/sinh(n pi r) < 0. Absolutely convergent along every ray 0<theta<pi/2 (rate a cos th + b sin th), ray independent, homogeneous of degree 1, Re g<0, Im g<0. Agreement with the ray integral to 1e-24 at ten (a,b) (`s2_mass_term.py`). This cuts the linear s-divergence of the 030 Maxwell tail at s ~ 1/f.
3. **But the M_str term is not the full resummed mass-linear term.** With all orders in the flux the vector mass multiplies the Zeeman-dressed resolvent: i int Theta [M_str + mu^2 (chi_V - 4)], chi_V = 2cosh 2x + 2cos 2y. The second part has the electric exponential growth of item 4.
4. **Order-F^4 character: no iε contour exists (exact growth law, numerically confirmed).** |Theta chi_str| ~ exp(|s| |a cos th - b sin th|) on s = |s| e^{-i th}; asymptotically Theta chi_str -> (ab/pi^2) sinh^2(s(a - ib)/2). On the real axis: exponential divergence for every f_e>0. The single ray th* = arctan(a/b) (on the leaf arctan(l/|y^4|)) is bounded, but the integrand tends to -ab/(2 pi^2), so the real part of int ds/s diverges logarithmically (-0.032 per doubling at a=0.7, b=1.3). Real masses (phases e^{-ism}) do not change the rate (`s3_character.py`).
5. **Leading-symbol A-dependence has no Einstein-Hilbert part and is not covariant (structural + numerical).** It enters only through the centre-valued flux: second variation = (1/2) D^2 g [Q,Q], Q = zeta_Y(k)(kappa∧alpha). Leaf-averaged at four orbit points: gauge invariant, indefinite (both signs on the quotient by kappa), not proportional to the covariant Maxwell structure (residual 0.4-1.0 at the two points with moderate f_e/f_m) (`s4_leaf_hessian.py`; the 028 closed form f_e/f_m = 1/sinh tau is reproduced to 5 digits at four points, all directions).
6. **Einstein-Hilbert sector in a Landau model (validated engine; a model, not the leaf).** Exact second order of Tr e^{-isH} for a plane-wave vielbein/gauge perturbation on the constant-F algebra (`engine.py`, `engine_mp.py`). Validated: Maxwell coefficient at F=0 (1e-10); 030's tensor A at F=0 with c1/A = i to 1e-8 in five draws; gauge invariance at F≠0 (2e-15); constant-field limits (1e-6). At F≠0 the ds/s coefficient c = A/16 pi^2 is flux independent; the integrand decays; the finite remainder Fin/c is 7.6, 8.1, 6.8 in three Lorentz frames of F at fixed (a,b)=(0.7,1.3) and 7.6, 5.9, 7.3 at (1.92,1.0); the Maxwell part varies by up to 40% between frames (`s7_resummed_sector_mp.py`). The flux converts the IR end of the 030 logarithm into a finite remainder of the size of the log that depends on the orientation of F: not covariant, and the sign follows A (indefinite).

Verdict on the three questions: (i) finite: the M_str term yes; the character term and the vector-Zeeman part of the mass term no (no contour). (ii) EH form: absent at the leading resummed symbol; in the Landau model the log coefficient is 030's A, unchanged. (iii) covariant and sign-definite: no, to the extent computed. No finite, covariant, sign-definite coefficient follows; no candidate missing link.

## Research and reasoning
**Set-up.** At the leading nilpotent symbol H_X = eta^{ab} omega_a omega_b with [omega_a,omega_b] = i F_ab(k), k the centre variable (kernel plane of sigma(Box)). Species operators H_i = H_X + Z_i + m_i with Z_bg a number times a species matrix, so every species shares Theta and differs by the Zeeman character (vector 2cosh2x+2cos2y; Dirac spinor 4 cosh x cos y; the exponents follow from the same F as the orbital algebra via D-slash^2 = Box + (1/2)Gamma^{ab}Z_ab and [omega,omega] = -Z). The species identity chi_str = 4(cos y - cosh x)^2 is checked to 1e-40.

**Orbital factor.** KMS: Tr[rho A B] = Tr[rho B(t=s) A] with omega(t)=e^{2tF eta}omega gives G = -iF M^T (1-M^T)^{-1}, G - G^T = iF (1e-16). The electric plane (real eigenvalues of F eta) gives 1/sinh, the magnetic plane 1/sin, in e^{-isH}.

**Mass term closed form.** Rotating the contour to s=-iu (poles of 1/sin(au), contour above them) gives Re g = pi b sum (-1)^n/sinh(n pi b/a); rotating the other way (poles s=n pi/b of 1/sin(bs) above the real axis) gives Im g = pi a sum (-1)^n/sinh(n pi a/b). Both confirmed numerically. (A principal-value integral first coded for Im g was wrong because of cancellation at u->0 and was dropped; the ray integral is the reference.)

**Character term.** On the lower half plane sin y ≈ e^{iy}/(2i), cos y ≈ e^{iy}/2, sinh x ≈ e^x/2, hence Theta chi_str -> (ab/pi^2) sinh^2((x - iy)/2); its growth rate is |a cos th - b sin th|, zero only at th* = arctan(a/b); the constant term of sinh^2 = (cosh - 1)/2 gives the log. In the upper half plane Theta itself grows (the Landau/inverted-oscillator sum does not converge), so no ray there is admissible. The growth is vector Zeeman (e^{2x}) against orbital decay (e^{-x}): net e^{x}. Scalars decay, spinors are marginal, ghosts decay. Real masses (phases), a spinor mass, and transverse magnetic fluxes in planes orthogonal to (0,1) cannot remove it (the e^{2x} vector term and the electric 1/sinh are unchanged); only a complex mass (width) larger than the rate, removal of the vector sector, or f_e = 0 would. (For the transverse-flux statement the argument is structural; the full 10D character was not computed.)

**A-dependence.** In the nilpotent frame (D_a, centre) the perturbation -eps alpha_a sin(phi) Y with Y central changes the frame determinant by a unit triangular matrix (no change of the Liouville volume nu) and the centre-valued flux by delta F = eps cos(phi) zeta_Y (kappa_a alpha_b - kappa_b alpha_a). So the leading coefficient's second variation is the Hessian of g(F), a function of the two invariants of F: F-dependent, non-analytic (degree -1), not a covariant Maxwell structure; the constant-field limit was verified against the exact engine (check C). The EH form needs the next-to-leading symbol. Caveat found here: on the leaf [W_a, Y] = -q_a Ham(y^4) is of order q and non-zero, and Y has a horizontal component in the splitting H + V'; the 030 frozen model (omega commuting, Y central) is therefore not the nilpotent model, and the Landau model of item 6 is a covariant 4D problem (charged scalar in a constant field with a plane-wave vielbein), not the leaf. Scales: with F ~ Lambda the flux cuts off s at ~1/Lambda while the window acts at ~1/Lambda^2, so the Maxwell-type term is enhanced by a power of Lambda over the EH logarithm (heuristic, not computed).

**Engine.** Omega_a = omega_a + eps sin(phi)(m_a^c omega_c + n_a). Second order: -i s <V2> - s^2 int_0^1 du (1-u) <V1(su)V1(0)>; exponentials moved to the left, e^{i s phi(tau)} e^{-i s phi(0)} = e^{-i kappa^T eta Phi kappa} e^{L}; Wick's theorem for the quasi-free state with e^L tilting; a multiprecision variant for large |s| (the kappa^2 coefficient is a difference of terms ~ e^{2a Re s}; the double-precision `s6` overflows at rho ~ 12).

**Literature.** Bastianelli-Davila-Schubert, arXiv:0812.4849 (JHEP 0903:086): abstract opened; a fetch summary of the ar5iv text states that the linear-in-curvature scalar-loop Lagrangian in constant F has no flux dressing of the R term beyond the prefactor, and flux-dressed R_{ab} and R_{abcd}FF terms. Equations not re-derived or verified; consistency pointer only, not cited in the block.

\paragraph{Conjugate-coordinate class: exact Landau (nilpotent) resummation of the leading symbol in the background flux, and its effect on the mass-supertrace and order-\(F^4\) terms (conditional; bounded no-go).}
Keep the setting of the preceding paragraphs: Poisson limit, leaf \(\mathbb{CP}^{1,2}\), \(T^a=M^{a4}\), \(H_X=-\Box_X\), the supplied content with no bilinear fermion mass, \(M_{\mathrm{str}}=4\mu^2+n_{\mathrm{ex}}\mu_i^2\), and the nilpotent (leading) symbol, in which the horizontal momenta \(\omega_a=\xi(W_a)\) obey \([\omega_a,\omega_b]=\mathrm iF_{ab}(k)\) with \(F_{ab}\) the constant flux at the centre variable \(k\) of the kernel plane of \(\sigma(\Box)\); \(\eta F\) has eigenvalues \(\pm f_e\) and \(\pm\mathrm if_m\). At four sampled orbit points the ratio \(f_e/f_m\) on the kernel plane equals \(\ell/|y^4|\) in every direction of \(k\) to five digits, which is the closed form of the kernel-plane flux above. Write \(a=f_e\), \(b=f_m\), \(x=sa\), \(y=sb\).

\emph{Orbital factor.} For \(H_0=\eta^{ab}\omega_a\omega_b\) the Gaussian (KMS) two-point function \(G=-\mathrm iFM^{T}(1-M^{T})^{-1}\), \(M=\mathrm e^{2sF\eta}\), gives, per unit horizontal volume, \(\Theta(s)=\operatorname{Tr}\mathrm e^{-\mathrm isH_0}=-\mathrm i\,ab\,\bigl(16\pi^2\sinh x\,\sin y\bigr)^{-1}\), with \(s\to s-\mathrm i0\) (the poles of \(1/\sin y\) lie above the contour, as for the Landau-level sum \(\tfrac{b}{2\pi}\sum_n\mathrm e^{-\mathrm isb(2n+1)}\)). The identity \(\mathrm d\ln\Theta/\mathrm ds=-\mathrm i\eta^{ab}G_{ab}\) holds to \(10^{-10}\) at complex \(s\), and \(\Theta\to-\mathrm i/(16\pi^2s^2)\) for \(s\to0\). The electric pair gives \(1/\sinh\) and the magnetic pair \(1/\sin\).

\emph{Mass-supertrace term.} The term \(+M_{\mathrm{str}}\operatorname{Tr}[(H_X-\mathrm i0)^{-1}]=M_{\mathrm{str}}\,\mathrm i\!\int_0^\infty\!\mathrm ds\,\operatorname{Tr}\mathrm e^{-\mathrm isH_X}\) becomes, per unit horizontal volume and per centre point, \(M_{\mathrm{str}}(16\pi^2)^{-1}(s_0^{-1}+g)\) with
\[
g(a,b)=\int_0^\infty\!\mathrm ds\Bigl[\frac{ab}{\sinh(as)\,\sin(bs-\mathrm i0)}-\frac1{s^2}\Bigr]=\pi\bigl[\,b\,S(b/a)+\mathrm i\,a\,S(a/b)\bigr],\qquad S(r)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{(-1)^n}{\sinh(n\pi r)}<0 .
\]
The integrand is analytic in the open fourth quadrant and bounded by \(\exp[-|s|(a\cos\vartheta+b\sin\vartheta)]\) on the ray \(s=|s|\mathrm e^{-\mathrm i\vartheta}\), so \(g\) is finite for \(a,b>0\) and independent of \(\vartheta\); the closed form follows from the two residue sums (poles of \(1/\sinh\) at \(s=-\mathrm in\pi/a\), poles of \(1/\sin\) at \(s=n\pi/b\)) and was confirmed against the ray integral to \(10^{-24}\) at ten pairs \((a,b)\). \(g\) is homogeneous of degree one, \(\operatorname{Re}g<0\) and \(\operatorname{Im}g<0\) for all \(a,b>0\), \(\operatorname{Re}g\to-a\ln2\) for \(b\ll a\) and \(\operatorname{Im}g\to-b\ln2\) for \(a\ll b\). Thus the linear divergence in \(s\) of the Maxwell tail \(c_0\) of the preceding paragraph is cut off at \(s\sim1/f\), and the flux supplies an exponentially convergent infrared end of the proper-time integral.

\emph{Completeness of the \(M_{\mathrm{str}}\) term.} With the flux kept to all orders, the mass-linear term is not \(M_{\mathrm{str}}\operatorname{Tr}[(H_X-\mathrm i0)^{-1}]\) alone: the vector mass multiplies the Zeeman-dressed resolvent, and the complete term is \(\mathrm i\!\int\!\mathrm ds\,\Theta(s)\bigl[M_{\mathrm{str}}+\mu^2(\chi_V-4)\bigr]\), \(\chi_V=2\cosh2x+2\cos2y\). The second part is the all-order version of the constant \(\sum_iw_im_i\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_i\Sigma_i\) listed above and has the growth of the next paragraph.

\emph{Order-\(F^4\) character.} With \(\chi_{\mathrm{str}}=4(\cos y-\cosh x)^2\) (the species sum \(2\cosh2x+2\cos2y+6-2-8\cosh x\cos y\), identity checked to \(10^{-40}\)) the resummed integrand is \(\Theta\chi_{\mathrm{str}}/s\). On the ray of angle \(\vartheta\) one has \(\bigl|\Theta\chi_{\mathrm{str}}\bigr|\sim\exp\bigl[|s|\,|a\cos\vartheta-b\sin\vartheta|\bigr]\) (measured rates agree to \(4\cdot10^{-3}\) at four angles away from \(\vartheta_*\)), and \(\Theta\chi_{\mathrm{str}}\to(ab/\pi^2)\sinh^2[s(a-\mathrm ib)/2]\) at large \(|s|\) (ratio \(1\) to \(10^{-8}\)). The exponential growth therefore occurs on the real axis (the \(\mathrm i\epsilon\) contour) for every \(a>0\), the same exponent holds for \(\Theta(\chi_V-4)\), the exponent is unchanged by the phases \(\mathrm e^{-\mathrm ism_i}\) of real masses, and it vanishes only on the single ray \(\vartheta_*=\arctan(a/b)\), which depends on the flux; on the leaf \(\vartheta_*=\arctan(\ell/|y^4|)\). On that ray the integrand is bounded, but it tends to the constant \(-ab/(2\pi^2)\) plus oscillations, so \(\int\mathrm ds/s\) grows logarithmically in the real part (numerically \(-0.032\) per doubling of the upper limit at \(a=0.7\), \(b=1.3\); the imaginary part converges). No ray gives a convergent integral, and the order-\(F^4\) term has no finite value from the proper-time representation of the class; its non-analytic dependence on \(f_e\) is that of a spectral sum over the complex eigenvalues \(\lambda\pm2\mathrm if_e\) of the vector sector and is not computed.

\emph{Dependence on \(A\).} The leading nilpotent coefficient depends on \(A\) only through the centre-valued flux, so its second variation is \(\tfrac12D^2g\,[Q,Q]\) with \(Q_{ab}=\zeta_Y(k)(\kappa_a\alpha_b-\kappa_b\alpha_a)\); it contains no second derivative of the fluctuation and no term of Einstein--Hilbert form. At four orbit points, averaging over the direction of \(k\) with the window of \(\Delta_{3/4}\), this form is gauge invariant (\(\alpha\propto\kappa\) in its kernel) but is not proportional to \((\kappa\cdot\kappa)\eta^{ab}-\kappa^a\kappa^b\) (relative residual \(0.4\)--\(1.0\) in real and imaginary parts at the points with \(0.3\le f_e/f_m\le0.8\)), and its eigenvalues on the quotient by \(\kappa\) have both signs. The infrared-finite replacement of the Maxwell tail is therefore neither covariant nor sign-definite.

\emph{Einstein--Hilbert sector in a four-dimensional Landau model.} Let \(\Omega_a=\omega_a+\epsilon\sin\phi\,(m_a{}^c\omega_c+n_a)\), \(\phi=\kappa\cdot x\), \(m_a{}^c\kappa_c=0\), on the algebra above with \(F\) constant; the choice \(m_a{}^c=-\alpha_ay^c\), \(n_a=-\alpha_a c\) reproduces the sector of the preceding paragraph with the commutators \([\omega_a,\omega_b]=\mathrm iF_{ab}\) kept, but without the commutator of \(Y\) with \(W_a\) that the Jacobi identity fixes on the leaf, so it is a model and not the leaf. The second order in \(\epsilon\) of \(\operatorname{Tr}\mathrm e^{-\mathrm isH}\) was computed exactly by Wick's theorem in the state \(\mathrm e^{-\mathrm isH_0}\). It reproduces the Maxwell coefficient at \(F=0\) (ratio \(1\) to \(10^{-10}\)), the form \(A\) of the preceding paragraph at \(F=0\) (\(c_1/A=\mathrm i\) to \(10^{-8}\) in five random draws, including the dilaton term), gauge invariance in \(n\) at \(F\ne0\) (\(2\cdot10^{-15}\)), and the constant-field limits (Hessian of \(\Theta\) in \(F\), and \(|\det M|^{-1}\Theta(MFM^T)\) for a constant vielbein). At \(F\ne0\) the coefficient \(c=A/16\pi^2\) of \(\mathrm ds/s\) is unchanged, the integrand decays like \(\mathrm e^{-|s|(a\cos\vartheta+b\sin\vartheta)}\), and the finite part \(\mathrm{Fin}=\lim[\int_{s_0}^\infty T_2\,\mathrm ds-c\ln(1/s_0)]\), in units of \(c\), is about \(7.6\), \(8.1\), \(6.8\) in three Lorentz frames of \(F\) at \((a,b)=(0.7,1.3)\) (data \(\kappa,\alpha,y\) rotated, \(A\) fixed), and \(7.6\), \(5.9\), \(7.3\) in three frames at \((a,b)=(1.92,1.0)\). The flux therefore turns the infrared end of \(\int\mathrm ds/s\) into a finite remainder of the size of the logarithm that depends on the orientation of \(F\) relative to \(\kappa,\alpha,y\); the Maxwell part of the same sector changes by up to \(40\%\) between frames. The coefficient of the logarithm is the flux-independent \(A\) of the preceding paragraph.

This is a statement for the Poisson limit, one loop, the nilpotent (leading) symbol resummed in a constant flux at the centre variable, the Lorentzian \(\mathrm i\epsilon\) proper time, the supplied content, and numerical evaluation at sampled points; the closed form of \(g\) is checked numerically, not proved, and the Landau model for the Einstein--Hilbert sector is not the leaf.
