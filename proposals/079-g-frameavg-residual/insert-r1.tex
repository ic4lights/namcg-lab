\emph{Frame-uniformity of the finite-N residual bound.} Under the hypotheses of the frame-average paragraph, the stacked-rows paragraph it extends and the row-perturbation paragraph behind them (compact factor with positive contraction, fixed frame, leading semiclassical order at the leading-order entropy, one diagram selected from the moments of the edge data, rows in two stacks of equal length with depths held fixed, saddle rows), the finite-N residual of the stacked fixed-frame defect against the continuum limit of the stacked-rows paragraph admits an explicit bound that is independent of the frame, and this content is now established with explicit constants rather than verified numerically. The bound is a sum of frame-free parts. Each row sum of logarithms of the multiplicative defect is bracketed between its integral over the row and the integral with both ranks shifted by the row index: the bracketing error is at most the endpoint logarithm of the ratio of the outer rank to the block rank of that row (Riemann bracketing, checked in exact integer arithmetic), and the rank-shift error is at most the row fraction times a constant of the rank fractions alone, by a mean-value bound on the logarithm. Each stacked-hook product telescopes exactly to a product of factors indexed by the rows of the second stack, and the total hook contribution is at most the product of the two depths times the logarithm of the first row length plus the sum of the depths (exact telescoping, checked in exact rational arithmetic). The multiplicative defect is the exact product of content factors and hook ratios of the stacked-rows paragraph, verified against the hook-content dimension ratio in exact rational arithmetic on small cases. The resulting bound, the product of the depths times log N plus a constant of the rank fractions and depths alone, involves the frame in no way; and since the defect depends on the frame only through the row lengths, and the row lengths of valid diagrams never exceed the total rank, the bound holds for every frame at every N. Hence the residual bound of the frame-average paragraph, previously verified numerically on frames with N up to 3200, is frame-uniform in the stated class, the residual per N tends to zero uniformly over frames, and the uniform-in-N residual bound of the row-perturbation paragraph and of its stacked extension is established. A sampled frame scan confirms the inequality margin at the shipped scale: at N=3200 with symmetric block ranks and saddle parameter one, the worst residual over the sampled frames is 7.1 for two rows and 54.9 at depth three, against frame-free bounds 8.8 and 77.0, and the bound takes one and the same value over the sampled frames at fixed rows (numerical, sampled frame grids). These statements are for the dominant-representation class of the three paragraphs named above with depths held fixed; they bound the bracketing, rank-shift and hook errors without optimising the constants, they leave the selection of the diagram, the monotonicity statement, the minimisation over frames and the two-sided diagrams untouched, and they close nothing in Problem~G.
