\emph{Uniformity locus of the profile, the frame and the induced coupling.} Within the named coupled gaussian slot law of the preceding paragraph - slots with variances \(s^{2}g_{i}\), unit frame \(u\), \(\mu\) and \(\Gamma\) as defined there - and for profiles at fixed \(D\) and \(W=\sum_{i}g_{i}\), three requirements of the surrounding paragraphs share exactly one profile: decoupling of \(a\) and \(r\) at a Haar-typical frame, minimality of the fixed-\(D\) obstruction of the third paragraph above, and vanishing of the frame-averaged induced coupling of the preceding paragraph. For a frame with \(m\) nonzero entries the criterion of the preceding paragraph gives independence of \(a\) and \(r\) if and only if \(g\) is constant on the frame support; a frame of full support - in particular every Haar-typical frame - therefore decouples \(a\) and \(r\) if and only if \(g_{i}=W/D\) for every slot, while a frame with \(m\le D-1\) nonzero entries constrains \(g\) only on its support: on that constancy slice \(\sum_{i}g_{i}^{2}\) is at least \(W^{2}/D\), attained only at the uniform profile, reaches \(W^{2}/\min(m,D-m)\) on the equal-complement subfamily, and can attain \(W^{2}\) overall, so the fourth-moment failure \(\rho_{2}-1=1/D_{\mathrm{eff}}\) of the fourth paragraph above can still attain the single-row value \(1\). Averaged over Haar frames, the coupling of the preceding paragraph obeys \(\mathbb{E}_{u}\Gamma=(D\sum_{i}g_{i}^{2}-W^{2})/(D(D+1))=W^{2}(D_{\mathrm{eff}}^{-1}-D^{-1})/(D+1)\), a function of the profile through the effective dimension of the fourth paragraph above alone: it vanishes if and only if \(g\) is uniform, and the coupled fourth-moment excess \(128s^{4}\Gamma\) of the preceding paragraph averages to \(128s^{4}W^{2}(D_{\mathrm{eff}}^{-1}-D^{-1})/(D+1)\). Hence the uniform profile is the unique common locus of decoupling at a Haar-typical frame, zero frame-averaged coupling, and the sharp fixed-\(D\) minimum of the third paragraph above, whose floor \(\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}(1+k/D)\) tends to one only as \(D\to\infty\). A selection reading is conditional: it would require the state-selection side of the matrix model to drive the induced law to decoupling at a Haar-typical frame at fixed \(D\) and fixed \(W\), an assumption nothing in the repository supplies. The locus condition is also strictly stronger than the recovery regime of the fourth paragraph above, which needs only \(D_{\mathrm{eff}}\to\infty\): the integrated fuzzy variance proxies \(f=\nu l/2\) of the fourth paragraph above have strictly increasing profiles, so their effective dimension stays strictly below the row length at every cutoff and they remain off the uniformity locus while reaching the recovery regime through growth of that dimension; their frame-averaged coupling vanishes as the slot count grows, so averaged decoupling does not imply uniformity at fixed \(D\). These statements hold within the named coupled gaussian slot law of the preceding paragraph, the averaged identities with Haar-distributed frames and the locus bounds for every finite profile and frame; they supply a locus condition on the profile rather than a selection mechanism, they do not fix the state that sets \(W\), \(g\), \(s\) or the frame \(u\), leave the missing steps (i)-(iv) of the Missing step paragraph unchanged, and they close nothing in Problem~G.
