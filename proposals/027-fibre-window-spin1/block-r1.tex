\paragraph{Conjugate-coordinate class: Lorentzian moments in the fibre-bounded cone window, and the vector, ghost and spin terms of the invariant sector (conditional; bounded no-go).}
Keep the setting and notation of the two preceding paragraphs (Poisson limit, leaf \(\mathbb{CP}^{1,2}\), \(T^a=M^{a4}\), \(\Box\), \(H_V\) with \(\mu^2=-3\), \(C_{42}\), \(C_{41}\), \(C_{31}\), \(\sigma(\cdot)\), \(S^2=2C_{41}-C_{42}\) with symbol \(\beta q_f\), the base symbol \(\alpha q_b\), \(a=\sinh\tau=y^4/\ell\), and the functions \(g\) and \(Q(a)\) of the exact invariant sector). At a point of the leaf take coordinates \((u,\zeta)\in\mathbb R^4\times\mathbb R^2\) on the cotangent space with \(|u|^2=\alpha q_b\) and \(|\zeta|^2=\beta q_f\), so that \(-\sigma(C_{42})=|u|^2-|\zeta|^2\), and write \(S\) for the form \(\sigma(\Box)\) (rank four, signature \((3,1)\)) in these coordinates.

\emph{Gaussian windows.} Let \(\lambda_0=-c\) and \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\) be positive and \(\mathcal M=\mathrm i\int_0^\infty\mathrm ds\prod_{k=0}^3(1+\mathrm is\lambda_k)^{-1/2}\), the Gaussian average of \((\sigma-\mathrm i0)^{-1}\). Rotating \(s=-\mathrm iu\) (the fourth quadrant contains no singularity) gives \(\mathcal M=\int_0^\infty\mathrm du\,(1-cu-\mathrm i0)^{-1/2}\prod_{k\ge1}(1+\lambda_ku)^{-1/2}\), whose first factor is real for \(u<1/c\) and equals \(\mathrm i(cu-1)^{-1/2}\) for \(u>1/c\). With \(u=(1\mp v^2)/c\),
\[
  \operatorname{Re}\mathcal M=\frac2c\int_0^1\!\mathrm dv\prod_{k\ge1}\Bigl(1+\frac{\lambda_k(1-v^2)}c\Bigr)^{-1/2}>0,\qquad
  \operatorname{Im}\mathcal M=\frac2c\int_0^\infty\!\mathrm dv\prod_{k\ge1}\Bigl(1+\frac{\lambda_k(1+v^2)}c\Bigr)^{-1/2}>0 ,
\]
and \(\operatorname{Re}\mathcal M=2/(\lambda+c)\) for \(\lambda_k=\lambda\). The pencil of \(\sigma(\Box)\) with any positive definite window form has one negative, three positive and two zero generalised eigenvalues, so the principal-value mass moment of every Gaussian window of the class is positive; this proves, for Gaussian windows, the positivity that the preceding paragraph found by sampling.

\emph{Geometry of the cone window.} At \(60\) random orbit points (agreement \(10^{-9}\) or better, not proved): the eigenvalues of the \(u\)-block \(S_{uu}\) are \(-\tfrac12\) and \(\tfrac12\sinh^2\tau\) (three times); the Schur complement of \(S_{uu}\) in \(S\) vanishes, so \(\sigma=Q_L(u+w\zeta)\) with \(Q_L=S_{uu}\) and \(w=S_{uu}^{-1}S_{u\zeta}\); \(w\) is conformal, \(|w\zeta|=\coth\tau\,|\zeta|\), and its image is orthogonal to the time eigenvector of \(Q_L\). In the variable \(x=u+w\zeta\) the removed region \(|u|<|\zeta|\) is therefore the ball of radius \(r=|\zeta|\) about the spacelike point \(p=w\zeta\), at distance \(r\coth\tau=r\sqrt{1+c/\lambda}\) from the apex \(x=0\) with \(c=\tfrac12\), \(\lambda=\tfrac12\sinh^2\tau\): the cone \(|u|=|\zeta|\) touches the null cone of \(Q_L\).

\emph{Mass moment in the fibre-bounded cone window.} Let \(\chi\ge0\) be bounded and integrable on \([0,\infty)\) and take the window function \(\chi(|u|^2-|\zeta|^2)\) on \(|\zeta|^2\le Q_f\) and \(|u|\ge|\zeta|\). For a ball of radius \(R\) about \(p\) with \(|p|<R\), polar coordinates about the apex give \(\operatorname{PV}\!\int_{|x-p|<R}\mathrm d^4x\,Q_L^{-1}=\tfrac12(R^2-|p|^2)I_0+p^{\mathsf T}\mathsf Jp\), where \(I_0=\operatorname{PV}\oint\mathrm d\Omega\,Q_L^{-1}=8\pi^2/\cosh^2\tau\) and \(\mathsf J=\operatorname{PV}\oint\mathrm d\Omega\,nn^{\mathsf T}Q_L^{-1}\), because the remaining term of the exit radius is odd in the direction \(n\) and \(Q_L\) is even (checked against a direct quadrature to \(10^{-4}\)). For spacelike \(p\) the same expression holds up to the tangency distance \(R\coth\tau\): this follows from the unique continuation of the solution \(\operatorname{PV}\!\int_{|x-p|<R}Q_L^{-1}\) of the wave equation of \(Q_L\) from the ball \(|p|<R\) to the domain of dependence, and was checked by quadrature to \(10^{-3}\) (it fails beyond tangency); it is argued, not proved here. Granting it, every shell \(r\le|u|\le\sqrt{r^2+\Lambda^2}\) has principal-value moment exactly \(\tfrac12\Lambda^2I_0\), independent of \(p\), and so
\[
  \operatorname{Re}\langle(\sigma-\mathrm i0)^{-1}\rangle_W=\tfrac12\,\mathcal J_c\,\pi Q_f\,I_0\int_0^\infty\chi(\lambda)\,\mathrm d\lambda>0 ,
\]
with \(\mathcal J_c>0\) the Jacobian of the coordinates \((u,\zeta)\); the real part depends on the cutoff function only through \(\int\chi\) and on the fibre bound only through the area \(\pi Q_f\). The imaginary part is \(\pi\int_W\chi\,\delta(\sigma)\ge0\). The moment is finite: the apex singularity is of order \(|x|^{-2}\) in four dimensions and the cone singularity is a simple pole against a Lipschitz window function.

\emph{Zeeman field and electric growth.} The first-order term of \(H_V\) is exactly the Zeeman matrix of the field \(F_{ab}(\xi)=\xi(\mathcal J_{ab})\), linear in the covector \(\xi\); let \(\eta F\) have eigenvalues \(\pm A\) (electric pair, factor \(\mathrm e^{\pm2sA}\) in \(\mathrm e^{-\mathrm isH_V}\)) and \(\pm\mathrm iB\) (magnetic pair, phases), so \(A^2-B^2=-F_{ab}F^{ab}/2=-\sigma(C_{31})(\xi)\). At the same \(60\) points (\(10^{-9}\)): on the base plane \(B=0\) and \(\max A=|u|\cosh\tau/\sqrt2\); on the vertical plane of the pencil \(A=|\zeta|\sinh\tau/\sqrt2\), \(B=|\zeta|/\sqrt2\) in every direction; on the kernel plane of \(S\) (the centre variable of the nilpotent model) \(A^2=|\zeta|^2/(2(1+2\sinh^2\tau))\) and \(B^2=|\zeta|^2\sinh^2\tau/(2(1+2\sinh^2\tau))\), so \(F_{ab}F^{ab}/2\) changes sign exactly at \(|y^4|=\ell\). The fibre-driven electric rate \(2A\) is thus equal to \(2s_f\sinh\tau\) with \(\beta q_f=2s_f^2\le2s_{\max}^2\), bounded in the window independently of the base cutoff, whereas the base-driven rate grows linearly with \(|u|\); the determinant stays finite, \(\ln\det(\sigma+Z)=\ln(\sigma^2-4B^2)+\ln(\sigma^2+4A^2)\), so the growth is a property of the Schwinger representation, bounded in the window and not removed by the fibre bound.

\emph{Vector and ghost sectors of the invariant sector.} The ghost operator is \(-\Box\), the same as the scalar of the preceding paragraph. On fibre-constant vector fields \(A_c(y)\) the cross term \(\eta_{ac}\eta_{bd}\mathcal J^{ab}\Sigma^{cd}\) of \(C_G\) equals \(-2s\mathcal J_{ab}A^b\) exactly (symbolic check), so \(H_V=-\Box-2s\mathcal J_{ab}A^b+\mu^2\) is \(C_G-C_{41}-C_\Sigma+\mu^2\) there. For the exponent \(t\Delta_{H^4}-\mathrm isH_V\) of the windowed Schwinger factor on the four components, the first Seeley--DeWitt coefficient, with the \(4\times4\) matrix connection, is
\[
  \operatorname{tr}\bigl(E+\tfrac16R\bigr)=4D_1+\frac{6s^2(1+a^2)}{t+\mathrm isa^2}-4\mathrm is\mu^2,
\]
\(D_1\) the scalar first correction of the preceding paragraph: the spin term \(2s^2\operatorname{tr}[G\,\sigma(\Delta_{H^3})]=-2s^2\operatorname{tr}[G\,\sigma(C_{31})]\), \(G\) the inverse of the symbol of the exponent, is the Zeeman square \(A^2-B^2\) traced against \(G\). The route reproduces the exact kernel coefficient \(D_1\) of the scalar to \(120\) digits at three points, and the vector and Dirac traces were verified to the same accuracy; the vector kernel itself was not computed. With \(x=s/t\) and the subtraction of the preceding paragraph, the weight-two functions are \(Q_\mu=-\mu^2\mathcal J_a\), \(\mathcal J_a=2(1+\mathrm i/a)/(1+a^2)\), and \(Q_{\mathrm{sp}}=-8/(1+a^2)-\mathrm i\,4(3a^2+1)/(a^3(1+a^2))\) for the spin term, so \(\operatorname{Re}[Q_{\mathrm{sp}}+4Q_{\mu}]=16/(1+a^2)\) for \(\mu^2=-3\). For \(n_{\mathrm{ex}}\) further scalars of mass \(\mu_i^2\) the bosonic content has
\[
  \operatorname{Re}Q_{\mathrm{bos}}=(2+n_{\mathrm{ex}})\operatorname{Re}Q+\frac{16-2n_{\mathrm{ex}}\mu_i^2}{1+a^2},
\]
plus the scale-dependent term proportional to \(2+n_{\mathrm{ex}}\) of the preceding paragraph. The first term is indefinite and the second has the sign of \(8-n_{\mathrm{ex}}\mu_i^2\); the scale-dependent term has the sign of a logarithm. On the full leaf the spin term in a Gaussian window has a large-\(s\) tail \(C\,\mathrm ds/s\) with \(C\) purely imaginary and proportional to \(\sigma(C_{31})\) on the kernel plane, that is to \((1-a^2)/(1+2a^2)\), so its logarithmic coefficient is of indefinite sign on the leaf, and its real part depends on the contour angle.

\emph{Supertrace.} The matrix part of the coefficient is traceless, so it enters only through \(\operatorname{tr}\Sigma^{ab}\Sigma^{cd}\), and the species-independent part \(R/6+E\) is multiplied by the number of components. For the Dirac representation \(\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_D\Sigma_D=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_V\Sigma_V\) for all index pairs. Supply, as an input not contained in the class, the sixteen real fermion components of the ten-dimensional supersymmetric matrix model with the square of the Dirac operator equal to \(\Box+s\Sigma^{ab}\mathcal J_{ab}\) on each component, no bilinear mass, and \(n_{\mathrm{ex}}=6\), and weight \(-\tfrac12\) per real fermion component. Then the component count \(4+6-2-8\) vanishes, the weighted index \(1-\tfrac12\cdot4\cdot\tfrac12\) vanishes, the Zeeman character is \(4(\cos sB-\cosh sA)^2=s^4(A^2+B^2)^2+\dots\) (the order \(s^4\) is a sum of the squares of the two invariants and does not vanish for any non-zero flux in Lorentzian signature), and what remains of the weight-two coefficient in the invariant sector is \(-\sum m^2\) weighted by statistics times \(\mathcal J_a\): the mass supertrace \(4\mu^2+n_{\mathrm{ex}}\mu_i^2\) times the positive mass moment. The mode-count term and the spin term of the order \(F^2\) both cancel identically; the curvature and connection part of the weight-two term therefore does not carry the Newton coefficient for this content, which is then a property of the next order of the expansion.

This is a statement for the Poisson limit, one loop, the leading symbol and the invariant sector, the four-matrix class with \(\mu^2=-3\), Gaussian windows and the cone window of the stated form, the sampled points and the supplied fermion content. The tangency extension of the shell identity is argued, not proved; the vector kernel and the second variation under \(A\) are not computed; fermions are not part of the class; the Jacobian \(\mathcal J_c\) and the Liouville normalisation are not computed as functions of position. It does not treat Moyal terms or a window built from dynamical matrices, it identifies no induced metric with the emergent metric of Chapter~\ref{ch:vierbein-emergence} and, since a Euclidean coefficient is not a Lorentzian one, leaves Assumption~\ref{ass:lorentzian} in place (Problem~E). This changes no ledger label; Problem~F remains open.
